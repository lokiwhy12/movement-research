# Movement DNA Taxonomy (v0)

Purpose: define the full extraction schema for the movement-research mission.

Mission context: this is **not** an Apotheosis design doc. It is a mechanics map for understanding the DNA of cult-like / movement-grade systems: what they are, how they work, why they feel compelling, when they spread, and how the same mechanics can become either durable or abusive.

---

## Core research question

What recurring mechanics make a movement feel:
- interesting
- irresistible
- identity-shaping
- loyalty-producing
- charisma-amplifying
- zeal-generating
- self-spreading
- durable over time

And just as important:
- when do those mechanics fail?
- when do they turn coercive, extractive, or self-destructive?

# 4) Ritual System — what gets embodied and repeated

Rituals are not a side feature. They are the engine that converts doctrine into memory, belonging, obedience, and replication.

## 4.1 Micro-rituals
Small recurring forms:
- greetings
- scripts
- closing lines
- check-ins
- daily checklists
- brief prayers / chants / mantras
- body cues / gestures

### Why they work
- reduce ambiguity
- create familiarity
- build rhythm
- make the worldview feel lived instead of abstract

## 4.2 Threshold rituals
First serious crossing events.

### Why they work
- create liminal break from ordinary life
- make commitment feel real
- generate high-salience memory
- bind emotion to identity change

## 4.3 Recurring maintenance rituals
What keeps the movement alive day-to-day or week-to-week?
- classes
- meetings
- workouts
- calls
- circles
- testimonies
- journaling / tracking / posting
- prayer / devotion / reading

### Why they work
- stabilize identity
- create habit loops
- prevent drift
- keep belonging active

## 4.4 Synchrony rituals
Events where many people do the same thing at once.
- group chants
- classes
- keynotes
- worship
- call-and-response
- annual challenge windows

### Why they work
- create emotional contagion
- intensify belonging
- produce “we are one thing” feeling

## 4.5 Status rituals
How status is made visible and felt.
- rank promotions
- sashes / badges / credentials
- graduations
- leaderboards
- titles
- testimonials from stage
- access to advanced rooms

### Why they work
- make hierarchy legible
- reward imitation
- increase aspiration and retention

## 4.6 Sacrifice / ordeal rituals
Pain, abstinence, endurance, money, confession, or exposure.

### Why they work
- costly signal of seriousness
- intensify cognitive commitment
- separate casual from committed
- make the experience unforgettable

## 4.7 Distribution rituals
Ritualized recruiting / evangelism.
- invite assignments
- testimony nights
- missionary door-knocking
- friend-bring weeks
- content posting
- public declarations

### Why they work
- turn distribution into identity
- convert members into operators
- collapse “marketing” into “practice”

# 11) Success Conditions — when the movement catches fire

## 11.1 Individual conditions
People are more susceptible / receptive when they are in:
- loneliness
- transition
- breakup / grief
- status collapse
- identity confusion
- spiritual hunger
- ambition without structure
- boredom / numbness
- post-success emptiness
- need for discipline / certainty / belonging

## 11.2 Environmental conditions
Movements spread more easily in:
- atomized cities
- low-trust institution climates
- high digital alienation
- economic precarity
- cultural fragmentation
- abundance of information + scarcity of meaning
- spectacle-driven media environments
- eras of disrupted identity roles

## 11.3 Movement-side conditions
A movement is more likely to succeed when it has:
- compact doctrine
- strong threshold ritual
- visible status carrier
- repeated practice
- operator layer
- distribution built into practice
- emotionally memorable experiences
- legible advancement

# 13) Failure + Collapse Conditions

Movements fail when they have some mix of:
- weak rituals
- vague doctrine
- no repeatable practice
- no operator layer
- no visible advancement
- too much founder dependence
- scandal that breaks legitimacy
- unsustainable cost / sacrifice demands
- incoherent identity story
- inability to reproduce locally
- purity spirals that cannibalize the base

## Bottom line

The core DNA is not “charismatic leader + followers.”

It is a tighter stack:
- compelling promise
- threshold ritual
- installed language
- repeated practice
- visible status
- social witness
- authority legibility
- built-in distribution
- and conditions that make people hungry for all of the above
