## Executive summary

Synanon sold a portable salvation product for people AA would not fully take: **cold-turkey residential containment**, a **peer “anticriminal society”** that renamed addicts as salvageable workers, and **The Game** — a timed, no-violence-allowed verbal attack circle that reframed humiliation as honesty and belonging. The adhesion engine was not “rehab beds” alone. It was a stack: withdrawal on the couch → Game catharsis → house labor hierarchy → (after ~1968) **no graduation / lifetime membership** → Squares paying for the Game as utopia → Church posture for tax and licensing escape → paramilitary Imperial Marines when critics and splittees threatened the container.

**Movement-DNA read (this pass):** Synanon fused **AA-derived mutual aid**, **attack-therapy ritual as the central sacrament**, **founder-as-Dad absolutism**, **business-as-mission (specialty advertising / “The People Business”)**, and — in the late phase — **violence and reproductive control as loyalty tests**. The durable afterlife is not the commune: it is the **therapeutic-community (TC) and troubled-teen industry lineage** (Daytop, Phoenix House, CEDU, Straight Inc., and copies) that kept confrontational “breaking” while dropping Synanon’s brand. That split — **method continuity + institutional amnesia** — is itself a durable mechanic (Mother Jones / LA Mag paraphrase).

**Public record (paraphrase, not our finding):** In October 1978, Synanon members placed a de-rattled rattlesnake in attorney Paul Morantz’s mailbox; he was bitten and hospitalized. Dederich and two associates pleaded no contest to conspiracy / assault-related charges; Dederich received probation and was barred from managing Synanon. IRS revoked 501(c)(3) status (effective back to 1977) amid findings framed around terror/violence policy and private enrichment; Synanon dissolved in 1991 after ordered back taxes reported around $17 million. **Needs counsel review. Not a legal conclusion of this packet.**
