Compact narrative

Business model: quiz/lead magnet → signature live weekend → multiple follow-on programs and coaching; revenue via paid trainings.

- Experiential weekend (20/60/20 pedagogy ratio)
- Alumni-as-conversion bridge

- Mitigation: clear scope & expectations, transparent outcomes, clinical referral for non-leadership issues.

2) Experiential weekend & pedagogy ratio
- Causal chain: high experiential time (60%) + sharing (20%) → embodied learning + rapid bonding → higher perceived transformation and follow-on sales
- Evidence:
  - "The pedagogy ratio itself (20% lectures / 60% experiential / 20% sharing) is a signal that the organization wants to be perceived as engineered transformation rather than generic coaching." — /home/ubuntu/.openclaw/families/loki/shared/docs/targets/movement-unfiltered-reaudit-v1.md
- Measurement plan:
  - Signal 1: immediate post-weekend self-reported outcome (effect size expected medium-large in short term, but watch decay over 3 months).
  - Signal 2: % enrolling in follow-on programs within 90 days (expect meaningful funnel conversion if experiential quality high).
- Risk severity: medium (emotional intensity can cause vulnerability; pressure to conform).
- Mitigation: informed consent, clear opt-outs, mental-health referral, alumni support.

3) Alumni-as-conversion bridge
- Causal chain: alumni advocacy → social proof + human warming for prospects → higher close rates

  - "Speak to an alumni" is an explicit referral mechanism in the conversion path." — /home/ubuntu/.openclaw/families/loki/shared/docs/targets/movement-unfiltered-reaudit-v1.md

  - Signal 1: prospect-to-customer conversion when alumni are engaged vs standard funnel (expect strong lift).
  - Signal 2: alumni net promoter participation rate.
- Risk severity: low-medium.
- Mitigation: alumni training for ethical conversion, clear compensation disclosure if any incentives exist.

Ethically-usable takeaway (1 line)
- Use self-diagnosis quizzes and alumni bridges to improve fit and conversion, but codify consent, scope, and clinical referrals to avoid overclaiming psychological change.
