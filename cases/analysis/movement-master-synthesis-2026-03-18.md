Prioritized safe experiments (ranked)
1) Compressed vocabulary A/B
- Goal: test whether short branded distinctions increase retention and referral without increasing harm.
- Design: randomize incoming cohort (n=~200 each) to (A) program with 6 branded distinctions vs (B) program with neutral explanatory language. Primary metric: 90-day follow-on enrollment. Safety: consent language, opt-out for public sharing.
- Expected signal: small-to-medium uplift in share & follow-on (~+10–25%).

2) Optional public witness vs private reflection
- Goal: measure recruitment and retention impact of voluntary public sharing.
- Design: recruit intro cohorts; randomly offer public-sharing slots with explicit consent vs private journaling. Metrics: invites sent within 30 days; participant wellbeing survey at 14 days.
- Safety: mandatory debrief, aftercare link, opt-out enforced.
- Expected signal: public sharing increases invites but may increase short-term vulnerability reports.

3) Daily micro-ritual scaled test
- Goal: test habit strength & retention from a small daily ritual with optional intensity scaling.
- Design: randomize to (A) strict checklist vs (B) scaled checklist; measure 30/90-day completion rates and adverse event reports.
- Safety: medical disclaimers; optional scaling; immediate stop-and-get-help guidance.
- Expected signal: strict increases short-term adherence but scaled track improves long-term retention and lowers harms.

4) Operator ladder transparency test
- Goal: compare transparent competency-based credentialing vs prestige-visible markers.
- Design: two affiliates implement either (A) transparent rubric/competency assessments or (B) visible rank markers; measure new operator quality, retention, complaints over 12 months.
- Safety: independent audit of assessments.
- Expected signal: transparent competency reduces harms and sustains quality better long-term.

Redlined mechanisms — DO NOT IMPLEMENT (high-risk / illegal / coercive)
- Secret inner societies with sealed hierarchies (e.g., NXIVM's DOS) — risk: abuse, criminal exposure. Action: block and report any design that seeks secrecy as status. Required legal review: immediate; human-rights/legal counsel; whistleblower protections mandated.
- Instrumented confession used as leverage (e.g., institutionalized auditing where disclosures become leverage) — risk: blackmail, privacy breaches. Action: prohibit collection of coercive collateral; require legal/ethical clearance for any sensitive-data collection.
- Sexualized initiation or assigned sexual access as ascension mechanics — risk: exploitation, criminal liability. Action: DO NOT RECOMMEND; if any somatic/sexual practices are considered, require third-party safeguarding, full informed consent, rotating partners, and external oversight; prefer non-sexual alternatives.
- Payment-gated secrecy ladders with opaque criteria — risk: economic capture. Action: require transparent pricing, third-party dispute mechanisms, refund/cooling-off windows.
- Moralization of recruitment ("you must recruit or fail morally") — risk: social coercion. Action: recruitment must be framed as optional and voluntary; prohibit messaging that frames non-recruitment as moral failure.

Required governance & legal checklist for ANY design that touches high-charge domains
- Independent legal review mapping to jurisdictional criminal and civil obligations.
- Mandatory reporting, whistleblower channels, and external ombudsperson for complaints.
- Mental-health referral partnerships and crisis pathways embedded in the product.
- Independent audit for any credentialing or private-data handling process.
- Human-subjects-style pre-registration for experiments involving emotional disclosure; include harm monitoring and stopping rules.

Safe experimental templates (control vs variant example)
Template 1 — "Distinction" A/B test
- Population: n=400 new users; randomize 1:1.
- Control: standard explanatory language.
- Variant: 6 branded distinctions plus optional sharing prompts.
- Metrics: 30/90-day retention, invites sent, wellbeing self-report.
- Stopping rules: any serious adverse events >1% trigger pause & review.

Template 2 — "Public Witness" opt-in experiment
- Population: n=250 cohorts; randomize 1:1.
- Control: private journaling module only.
- Variant: optional public-sharing slot with explicit consent & moderator.
- Metrics: invites, follow-on sign-ups, adverse events.
- Safety: mandatory debrief; mental-health referral if distress reported.
