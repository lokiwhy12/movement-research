## Executive summary

The Farm sold a portable **peace-and-planet product**: psychedelic-era **telepathic oneness** reframed as moral obligation ("we won't make it unless everybody makes it"), a **Bible-communism household** (Acts 2:44–45) that pooled money and labor, and an outwardly directed **service stack** — midwifery, soy foods, Plenty relief, book publishing — that recoded dropping out as dropping *in* to hard work, marriage seriousness, vegan discipline, and "voluntary peasant" identity. The adhesion engine was not "free love." It was nearly the opposite of the libertine stereotype: Monday Night Class belonging → Caravan ordeal → Gate threshold → crew labor → Sunday service / "working it out" honesty → (for many) lifelong Farm identity even after exit.

**Movement-DNA read (this pass):** The Farm fused **open-class charismatic transmission**, **mobile pilgrimage bonding (Caravan)**, **total-income collectivism under a spiritual teacher framed as abbot**, **agreement culture** (vegan, nonviolence, anti-abortion sanctuary offer, marriage seriousness), **portable crafts that outlived the commune** (Spiritual Midwifery; soy cookbooks; Geiger counters; Plenty), and — uniquely among famous 1960s–70s communes that did not implode into crime or total dissolution — a **deliberate soft landing**: the 1983 Changeover stripped the founder of financial power, imposed dues, privatized most businesses, kept the land in trust, retired ~$1M debt, and reinvented the site as an ecovillage / midwifery pilgrimage / visitor economy. That split — **vision continuity + economic amnesia of full communism** — is itself a durable mechanic. Contrast with targets that ended in catastrophe: here the high-control peak *restructured* rather than exploded.

**Public record / public reporting (paraphrase, not our finding):** 1974: Stephen Gaskin and three others convicted for growing marijuana on Farm-related property; served ~1 year of a 3-year sentence; Grass Case religious-sacrament appeal path declined by higher courts (Wikipedia / Farm timeline). Changeover 1983: community vote / All Farm Meeting to decollectivize amid debt and recession (official Changeover page; Bates post-communal economics; Stevenson "Why The Farm Survived"). Stephen Gaskin died 1 July 2014 (NYT / Wikipedia). **Needs counsel review. Not a legal conclusion of this packet.**
