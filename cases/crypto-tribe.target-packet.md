## Executive summary

Bitcoin, on bitcoin.org, is an open-source peer-to-peer payment network and a new kind of money. Official homepage (opened): nobody owns or controls it; issuing and validating happen collectively; the design is public. Official FAQ (opened): a consensus network; Satoshi Nakamoto published a specification in 2009 and left in late 2010; identity of the inventor is as relevant as the identity of the person who invented paper; no one person or group decides protocol changes — BIPs are proposed, users/miners/node operators adopt. Official "you need to know" (opened): you are your own bank; there is no undo; bitcoin is not anonymous; payments are irreversible; price is volatile; treat it as a high-risk asset; never store money you cannot afford to lose; it is not an official currency; tax and legal mandates remain the user's. Official legal disclaimer (last amended July 5, 2016): the site is general information, not legal or business advice; investment can lead to loss; the website does not store, send, or receive bitcoins. Official FAQ also names the phrase "not your keys, not your coins" as the reason self-custody exists, and tells readers never to expect to get rich.

That is the protocol-education voice. It is not the tribe.

Interpreter-class maximalism, from pages opened this pass, is a later overlay. Vitalik Buterin's Ethereum Foundation blog (Nov. 20, 2014, opened) popularized "Bitcoin dominance maximalism": the idea that a multi-coin environment is undesirable, that launching yet another coin is wrong, and that Bitcoin's monopoly is both righteous and inevitable — distinct, he wrote, from simply wanting Bitcoin to work. Pierre Rochard's "Speculative Attack" (Nakamoto Institute, opened) says Bitcoin will not be eagerly adopted; it will be forced by economic reality; good money drives out bad; the end state is hyperbitcoinization ("your money is no good here"). Daniel Krawisz's "Hyperbitcoinization" (Nakamoto Institute, opened) frames a voluntary switch from an inferior currency to a superior one, faster than hyperinflation, ending in a world where the old system is unimaginable. Jameson Lopp's "A History of Bitcoin Maximalism" (opened) maps Bitcointalk to Reddit scaling wars to Twitter dunk culture to pandemic "lifestyle maximalism," and splits defensive consumer-protection maximalism from purity-test "toxic" maximalism. BTC Inc.'s Bitcoin 2027 conference (conference.b.tc, opened) sells a Bitcoin-only pilgrimage with a GA / Pro / Whale caste, wristbands, and a FAQ that says the event is bitcoin-only and highly encourages young people to participate in Hyperbitcoinization.

HODL, from the opened Bitcointalk thread (GameKyuubi, Dec. 18, 2013) and Bitcoin Magazine's "Art of the HODL" (Apr. 9, 2021), is a misspelled hold that became a conviction identity: you are a bad trader, so you do not sell; later, low time preference and "hold on for dear life." Orange-pill, from Bitcoin Magazine (Aug. 26, 2022) and Ledger Academy glossary (Jun. 4, 2024), is the Matrix-colored initiation verb: show someone the fiat problem, then the Bitcoin lens. Official bitcoin.org never uses those two nouns on pages opened this pass.

External reporting (Wikipedia Bitcoin, pointer-then-open; Wikipedia Satoshi Nakamoto, pointer-then-open; Cambridge CCAF Digital Mining Industry Report page, opened; NYT energy interactive 403 this pass): first decentralized cryptocurrency; genesis block Times headline as timestamp and bailout jab; about 20 percent of coins estimated lost; energy use contested; hodl named as a 2013 volatility term; Satoshi's wallet untouched and identity unknown; Craig Wright ruled not Satoshi in UK High Court 2024. Economists via Wikipedia: bubble, fad, Ponzi, "new gold" — contested, not findings.

Movement-DNA read from pages opened this pass: the adhesion engine is not a company and not a weekend seminar. It is a four-layer stack — (1) a vanished founder plus a public paper that can be pointed to without being the liturgy; (2) irreversible self-custody that makes leaving look like self-harm ("lost keys = gone forever"); (3) a memetic identity (HODL / orange-pill / not-your-keys) that converts a price crash into an ordeal you passed; (4) a dual enemy (fiat as the system, other coins as the heresy) plus an eschatology (hyperbitcoinization) that makes every drawdown a test of faith. Official bitcoin.org is the sober classroom. Interpreter culture is the church. External reporting is the weather report. Do not collapse them.

Labeled Ethereum contrast (one line, from a page actually opened): Vitalik's 2014 essay is the named foil that minted "maximalism" as a pejorative; this packet does not extract Ethereum as a second tribe.

This is research extraction. Claims unverified. Needs counsel review. No legal conclusions. Not a buy/hold/sell recommendation.
