## The simplest accurate model: “Reframe → Declare → Enroll → Reinforce”
Landmark appears to run a tight loop:
1) **Reframe perception** (distinctions: filters, context, story vs what happened)
2) **Name your pattern** (rackets: recurring complaints / unwanted persistence)
3) **Convert it into a possibility** (invent a future that isn’t dictated by past)
4) **Declare it publicly** (tell someone outside; be witnessed)
5) **Enroll others** (invites to Tuesday evening / introductions)
6) **Reinforce through repetition + social roles** (follow-on courses, graduate identity, volunteer ladder)

This loop creates both personal change and distribution.

## Core distinctions (official curriculum atoms)
From the official syllabus:
- **Already Always Listening™** — you are filtered by pre-existing notions.
- **Hidden Power of Context** — contexts invisibly govern action/possibility.
- **Vicious Circle™** — fusing what happened with your interpretation creates stuckness.
- **What happened vs the story** — separating fact from meaning restores degrees of freedom.
- **Rackets™** — complaint + payoff + cost; once seen, you can interrupt the pattern.

Source: https://www.landmarkworldwide.com/the-landmark-forum/syllabus

## Pedagogy (how they teach it)
A participant pedagogy breakdown describes four delivery modes:
1) **Lecture** (leader reads from a manuscript; uses board; adds personal examples)
2) **Public coaching** (attendees share “negative stories”; leader coaches live)
3) **Dyads** (private partner sharing; encouraged to rotate partners)
4) **Real-world application via outreach** (phone calls to people you have negative feelings toward; practice “completion”)

Source: https://reza-vaezi.medium.com/landmark-forum-structure-and-pedagogy-df509cca859f

**Key insight:** the learning isn’t just conceptual — it’s repeatedly *performed* (inside the room, then outside the room).

## Assignments (what behaviors they operationalize)
### Day 1
- Identify “rackets” (unwanted, persists) via complaints; list payoffs + costs.
- Share a “new possibility” with a non-participant by next morning; do it in a way that leaves them touched/moved/inspired.

Source: https://landmarkforumnews.com/anz/day1/ (PDF analyzed)

### Day 2
- “Genesis of identity” reflection: look back across life stages for moments of not belonging / being on your own; see adaptations.
- Share possibility again, with explicit truth about inauthenticity + its impact + the victory over the past.

Source: https://landmarkforumnews.com/in/day2/ (PDF analyzed)

### Tuesday evening (Day 5)
- Graduates invite friends/family to an evening with the leader; practical intent is exposure + conversion.

Source: https://landmarkforumnews.com/in/day3/

**Key insight:** assignments convert insight into (a) self-surveillance, (b) identity narrative, (c) public commitment, (d) recruitment/distribution.

## The container (environmental leverage)
- Official schedule emphasizes long days (9am–~10pm) over three days + evening.
- NYMag describes a classroom-like environment; leaving discouraged; signs implying you “may get the result but have no right to expect it” if you leave.

Sources: Landmark schedule page; NYMag profile.

**Function:** immersion + reduced context switching + heightened group cohesion.

---

## Psychological drivers (why it sticks)
1) **Vocabulary acquisition** → new “handles” for inner experience.
2) **Public coaching** → high-salience moments + social proof.
3) **Dyad rotation** → rapid bonding + repeated articulation of the new frame.
4) **Commitment devices** → share your possibility outside the room quickly.
5) **Identity upgrade** → “graduate” status + implied path forward.
6) **Distribution baked into pedagogy** → invites are part of the process, not an afterthought.

---

## Essence (1 paragraph)
The Landmark Forum is best understood as a high-intensity, time-compressed learning container that installs a small set of branded cognitive distinctions (filters, context, story vs what happened, rackets) and then forces rapid behavioral rehearsal: public articulation, partner sharing, and immediate outreach to outsiders. The result is a felt identity shift (“the past is past; I can invent a new possibility”), reinforced by social witnessing and a built-in distribution mechanism (invitations to Tuesday evening and introductions), which then feeds a progression ladder of follow-on programs.
