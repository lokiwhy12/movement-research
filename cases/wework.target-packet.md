## Executive summary

WeWork was founded in 2010 by Adam Neumann and Miguel McKelvey after they sold their stake in GreenDesk, an eco-coworking precursor. The first WeWork opened at 154 Grand Street in New York (S-1: early 2010; Wikipedia: first SoHo location opened April 2011 — date conflict recorded). For a decade the company sold desks as belonging: renters were members, staff were a community team, and the product was packaged as a worldwide platform that would elevate how people work, live and grow. In August 2019 The We Company filed an S-1 whose opening sentence called WeWork a community company committed to maximum global impact, with a mission to elevate the world's consciousness. The same filing reported 527,000 memberships, 528 locations, $3.3 billion run-rate revenue, a $4.0 billion committed-revenue backlog, $47.2 billion of future undiscounted minimum lease-cost payments, and net losses of $1.9 billion (2018) and $0.9 billion (H1 2019). Within six weeks the IPO was pulled, Neumann was ousted, and the quasi-movement's public myth failed a market stress test.

Movement-DNA read from pages opened this pass: the adhesion engine was not the desk. It was founder-scale charisma plus a membership identity plus a physical container (events, stairs, Community desk) plus a language system (We / member / community / space-as-a-service / superpowers) that let a long-lease real-estate arbitrage present as a tech-and-consciousness company. When the S-1 made the numbers and the related-party stack public, the packaging did not survive non-herd scrutiny. SoftBank later recapitalized; a 2021 SPAC listing followed; WeWork Inc. filed Chapter 11 in the District of New Jersey on 6 Nov 2023 and emerged 11 Jun 2024. Official 2026 wework.com has dropped the consciousness register. Current copy sells a smarter way to work, hospitality-driven experiences, and half a million members. Community is still a word. It is no longer the doctrine.

## 0b) Environmental conditions (why now / why it scaled)

3-7 condition to demand to promise:

1. Post-2008 empty commercial space plus a freelance/startup workforce → demand for short-commitment, designed desks that did not look like cubicles → official: space-as-a-service; better day at work for less (S-1).
2. Urban isolation / work-from-a-coffee-shop fatigue → demand to be around other ambitious people without joining a firm → official: culture of inclusivity and the energy of an inspired community; 2026: work alongside professionals; join community events.
3. Sharing-economy permission structure (Uber / Airbnb as the 2014-15 fundraising weather) → demand that offices be as flexible as a ride → official (S-1 and Brown): WeWork lumped itself with sharing even though it held the leases.
4. Meaning-hungry millennial labor plus founder-worship VC → demand that a workplace also be a purpose and a tribe → official 2019: make a life, not just a living; elevate the world's consciousness.
5. Enterprise hybrid-work later wave → demand to convert long HQ leases into flex → official 2026: enterprise-grade hybrid strategy; Fortune 500 flex-space report.
6. SoftBank / Vision Fund surplus capital → demand for a founder who would spend faster → external (Brown, Wikipedia): you aren't crazy enough; $47 billion paper peak.
7. 2019 public-market and 2023 office-downturn counters → demand that the myth survive disclosure and a rent recession → it did not; IPO pulled; Chapter 11 filed.

Meaning crisis named (official, implicit): ordinary office work is lonely, ugly, inflexible, and purpose-free. You are one membership away from a community that helps you live with purpose (S-1). 2026 official names flexibility and cost, not purpose.

Enemy / chaos named:
- Official 2019: static long-term leases; stuffy corporate culture; workplaces that do not humanize work.
- Official 2026: one-size-fits-all real estate; hidden costs; slow traditional lease.
- External: the founder's own extraction and volatility; the press; commercial-real-estate bear (Wiedeman quotes Neumann: we were criticized because we poked the bear). Official 2026 pages opened this pass do not name journalists or Neumann as the enemy.

Certainty / belonging / status offered: a keycard; a first-name Community team; events; the word member; in the Neumann era, proximity to a messianic operator and a $47 billion story. After collapse: a desk that still says WeWork on the glass.


## 1) Promise + positioning (the offer)

Explicit promise (1 sentence, official 2019): we are a community company that gives members flexible access to beautiful space, inclusivity, and the energy of an inspired community, and we will elevate how people work, live, and grow — ultimately, the world's consciousness (S-1 opening).

Explicit promise (1 sentence, official 2026): WeWork is a leading global real estate provider that empowers businesses through flexible workplace solutions, technology, and hospitality-driven experiences — a smarter way to work (About).

Implicit promise: you are not a tenant in a building. You are a member of We. Your loneliness, your ugly office, and your long lease are optional. Continuation (more locations, WeLive, WeGrow, enterprise) is how the feeling stays true.

Positioning markers (opened): space-as-a-service (S-1); member not customer (CNBC on S-1); 2026 Made for work. Built for business; half a million members; Community team; All Access 450-plus locations.

## 3) Transformation sequence (the protocol)

Representative arc from pages opened this pass. Not a universal member biography.

1. Aspirational contact — designed lobby, press, founder story, a friend's keycard. Goal: ordinary office is spiritually insufficient. Expected state: envy, loneliness.
2. Low-friction yes — On Demand day pass (from $25 global / $39 SF starting; 24-hour cancel refund). Goal: body in the room.
3. Threshold purchase — All Access month-to-month or 3/6/12-month commitment; or Dedicated Desk. Goal: recurring identity.
4. Initiation — first weekday booking, government ID, Community team, keycard. Goal: you are a member. Expected state: welcome, slight awe.
5. Daily container — hot desk, coffee, stairs, other members in view. Goal: belonging without a meeting.
6. Event / introduction — lunch-and-learn, networking, S-1-scale programming. Goal: the community is real because you were seen.
7. Escalation — more credits, dedicated desk, private office, second city, enterprise seats, (historically) WeLive / WeGrow. Official attribution: your team is growing; you need a smarter real-estate strategy.
8. Advocacy — case-study quote; broker referral; bringing the team onto All Access passes (Dialpad quote on the All Access page).
9. Stress / exit — cancel with five days' notice if month-to-month and off-commitment (Help); or ride a Commitment Term; or, at company scale, watch the operator fail a public market and a Chapter 11. No official shunning doctrine on pages opened.

### Working score
- Repeatability: 5/5 — the keycard-and-desk grammar restages globally.
- Speed: 5/5 to a felt win (same-day desk, first-week events); 2/5 to elevated consciousness.
- Durability: 2/5 — the identity did not survive founder-removal and bankruptcy at system scale. Individual desks still sell.
