## Executive summary

Clubhouse is a voice-first social network built by Paul Davison and Rohan Seth under Alpha Exploration Co. Official 2026 homepage: "join live voice chats." Official 2020 origin post (Wayback of blog.clubhouse.com/check-1-2-3): a network of open rooms you hop in and out of; you enter as audience, raise a hand, and speakers may invite you up. Official July 21, 2021 Opening Day post: out of beta, waitlist removed, open to anyone on iOS or Android. Official 2026 Terms (last updated July 22, 2026; effective February 20, 2026): Conversations now include Live Rooms, asynchronous Chats, and Voice Messages; users can form Houses that are Open or Closed; a paid Clubhouse Plus layer exists; the company records Live Rooms to investigate guideline and Terms violations.

Wikipedia (pointer-then-open): Fall 2019 Talkshow prototype; March 2020 iOS launch; invite-only; $12 million Series A from Andreessen Horowitz in May 2020 at a $100 million valuation; about 600,000 registered users by December 2020; about 2 million weekly actives in January 2021; Series B at a $1 billion valuation; Elon Musk / Mark Zuckerberg / Oprah rooms in February 2021; downloads from about 3.5 million (Feb 1) to 8.1 million (Feb 15); weekly actives over 10 million then a 21 percent drop across three weeks from late February to early March; invites listed on eBay at up to $400; Clubhouse Payments with Stripe on April 5, 2021; Twitter acquisition talks at $4 billion that did not close; Series C at a $4 billion valuation; Android May 2021; waitlist discarded July 21, 2021 with 10 million still waiting; April 2023 staff cut of half. CNBC (April 27, 2023): 14.2 million downloads by mid-April 2021, growth already flattened before a revenue model; founders quoted that the opened-up world made it harder to find friends on the app and to fit long conversations into daily life; "years of runway"; Clubhouse 2.0. TechCrunch same day: Davison had said headcount was close to 100 the prior October; activity had already been moving off "live podcast" broadcasting into private rooms.

Movement-DNA read from pages opened this pass: the adhesion engine was not a doctrine and not a paid ladder. It was a four-layer stack — (1) an invite that made entry itself a status object; (2) a room architecture that put a visible stage above a mute audience and made being pulled up the conversion; (3) drop-in / leave-quietly as a zero-ordeal yes that produced FOMO without a daily identity ritual once you closed the app; (4) celebrity rooms as borrowed destiny. Official voice in 2020 said the closed door was temporary capacity, not exclusivity. The market treated the closed door as the product. Opening Day removed the door. Copycat live-audio rooms landed on larger networks. The long-conversation container did not survive leaving the house. What remains in 2026 official copy is a free chat app plus a Plus SKU and coin IAPs.

## 0b) Environmental conditions (why now / why it scaled)

3-7 condition to demand to promise:

1. COVID lockdown and the sudden death of in-person rooms → demand for a place to talk for hours without a camera → official 2020: voice-only while folding laundry / commuting; rooms that leave you feeling better.
2. Social-isolation / Zoom fatigue → demand for serendipity that is not a scheduled tile → official 2020 hop-in-and-out rooms; official 2021: conversation is what makes us human and is hard to find when life is busy.
3. Invite-graph prestige in a year when parties were cancelled → demand for a status object you could screenshot → official: waitlist because the team was small. Market: eBay invites (Wikipedia).
4. Creator / celebrity surplus with nowhere to appear live → demand for a stage that felt like a backstage → Wikipedia / CNBC: Musk, Oprah, Zuckerberg rooms as the proof.
5. Cheap late-stage venture and next-social-network weather → demand for a consumer app that looked like a movement → CNBC: $100 million to $1 billion to $4 billion; Andreessen / Tiger / DST.
6. Android + open-invite + incumbent copycats as the counter-condition → demand that the closed room survive becoming a feature on Twitter/Spotify/Discord → it did not; Opening Day removed the door; Wikipedia lists the copy set.
7. Post-lockdown calendar return → demand that a one-hour-plus audio hang still fit a commute-and-office day → CNBC/TechCrunch quotation of founders: harder to find friends and to fit long conversations into daily life.

Meaning crisis named (official, implicit): text-and-camera social media leaves you performing and alone. Voice, without a face, is how you become a person with other people again (Check 1, 2, 3; Opening Day). 2026 App Store names community and "pass the time," not a crisis.

Enemy / chaos named:
- Official 2020-21: the foil is typed social media and the difficulty of real connection when life is busy. Official 2023 sentences as quoted by TechCrunch: remote living, empty scrolling, and Zoom meetings.
- Official Guidelines: harassment, hate, doxxing, stage-trolling, people who sell access.
- External: the Chinese state / Agora path (Verge/SIO layer); copycat incumbents; the opened-up world (CNBC). Official 2026 pages opened this pass do not name journalists, Elon, or Agora as the enemy.

Certainty / belonging / status offered: an invite; a seat in a room that might contain a celebrity; a raised hand that might become a stage; later, a House; later still, a Plus badge that lets you listen unseen.

## 3) Transformation sequence (the protocol)

Numbered arc from pages opened. Describe, do not instruct.

1. Contact — waitlist or a friend's leftover invite (2020-21); 2026 App Store / guest link. Goal: get a body into the graph. Mechanism: scarcity, then convenience. Expected state: curiosity, FOMO.
2. First room — hop in as audience. Goal: hear a live voice. Mechanism: hallway + friend-is-speaking. Expected state: eavesdropper thrill in the ordinary sense: you are in the room.
3. Felt gap — you are mute; they are on stage; a celebrity may be two rooms over. Goal: make silence feel like a not-yet. Mechanism: visible caste. Expected state: smallness, hunger.
4. Raise hand — the first public ask. Goal: convert lurker to candidate. Mechanism: one tap plus social risk. Expected state: exposure.
5. Invite-up — a moderator pulls you. Goal: identity shift in one gesture. Mechanism: selection by the already-chosen. Expected state: arrival.
6. Long hang / tuck-in — hours in one room (official 2020). Goal: time-as-belonging. Mechanism: no camera, no end-time. Expected state: intimacy, fatigue, I live here.
7. Invite someone else / club or House — Goal: turn the status object into a graph. Mechanism: leftover invites; later, House create. Expected state: patronage.
8. Orientation / Town Hall or creator Payments — Goal: institutionalize the week and the take-home. Mechanism: Wednesday/Sunday (Opening Day); Send Money (Payments post). Expected state: regularity, vocation.
9. Aftercare / decay — open-invite, copycats, calendar return. Goal (company): Clubhouse 2.0 and private rooms (press quotation). Expected state: the hallway is empty of your friends.

Commitment moment: the first invite accepted, then the first time you speak. Integration structure: follow the people from the room; join the Club/House; (2026) start a Chat so the conversation survives the live slot.

### Working score
- Repeatability: 5/5 as a night (anyone can drop). 2/5 as a year (the graph has to stay).
- Speed: 5/5 — stage in one session.
- Durability: 2/5 — official 2023 sentences as quoted: the long conversation no longer fits the day.
