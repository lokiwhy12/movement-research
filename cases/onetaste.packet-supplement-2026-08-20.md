## 1. What kind of case OneTaste is, for each lane

**Commitment (Kanter).** For-profit, historically mixed residential: workshops, urban-retreat houses, a steep paid ladder, then a digital/events successor (packet §0, §3, §8). Kanter’s opened pages treat commitment as an organizational problem in 1780–1860 utopian communities, success cut at 25 years (1972, Appendix p.260). OneTaste is not that sample. Continuance (prepaid ladder, later non-refund memberships) is the closest fit. Cohesion and control run past the intro without commune-ordering of existence (Ch.4 p.90).

**Conversion / network (Lofland–Stark / Stark).** Acquisition is a mix: media, free evenings, live demonstration, referrals, later Eventbrite / Eros (§0, §7). Network entry on Stark’s 1996 restatement (ties; open network vs social implosion), not a millenarian convert series. Religious-seekership and extra-cult-detachment do not map as written for modal intro.

**NRM interiors (Lifton / Harvey / Melton / Ransom).** The packet’s high-control scan is observational and contested, not a verdict (§13). Co-residence *was* present in the house/staff era (§6, §13 item 4) and is not the default 2026 digital-member condition. Harvey’s blurred boundaries — whole life entwined so leaving means leaving employer, housing, and social orbit (Harvey §§1–2) — are closer for house/staff than for a class-only buyer. Lifton’s milieu control is a 1950s thought-reform claim (Ch.22).

**Brand community / fandom (Muniz and O’Guinn / Kozinets / Arvidsson and Caliandro).** OneTaste sold a branded practice (OM) and later labels (Institute of OM, Eros, practitioner/coach). Brand community is “a specialized, non-geographically bound community, based on a structured set of social relationships among admirers of a brand” (Muniz and O’Guinn 2001). Houses, circles, demos, and the Connection Program are interacting, not a brand public. Official talk is wellness / attention-training / “not sex,” not Kozinets’ religion/myth as a finding that the product is a religion.

**Movement failure (Tarrow 2022 Ch.8 / Fillieule 2015).** Mismatch for Tarrow. A cycle of contention is “a phase of heightened conflict across the social system” (Tarrow 2022, print p.195). OneTaste is a company that sold a branded practice — not early-risers against a state, not a polity-wide cycle. Public-record sentences and a successor Institute of OM are not automatically a Tarrow cycle ending. Closed vs transformative is his fork for cycle endings (p.195); do not run it. Fillieule’s individual disengagement can be asked of leavers; field collapse is not recorded.

## 2. Answers the packet can support

### Commitment

**Q1.** Arrangements touch continuance, cohesion, and control as three problems (Kanter Ch.3 pp.86–89), not as three equally built commune solutions. Continuance: prepaid ladder, peak-ritual deposits, 2026 non-refund memberships (§8, §10, S7). Cohesion: circles, houses, partner reputation (§4b, §5, §6). Control: container/form as authority, founder-as-doctrine plus rulebook (§2, §13 items 2, 9; primitives 1, 16). Separable as problems.

**Q2.** Investment is codeable: money puts a stake in the next meeting; successful groups generally did not reimburse seceders (Ch.4 p.96); prepaid tuition and a 7-day-then-no annual window (S7) are the nearest analogues. Sacrifice is partly codeable: hours, ordeal, tuition, house labor — not abstinence, austerity, or celibacy as Table 1 coded them (p.95). Communion is partial: frequent contact in houses and circles (§5, §6), not communal property. Mortification is partial: frames; fear-inventory; “turned on” check-in (§5). Renunciation is partial: Muggles/Matrix mark in/out; house graph weakens outside ties (§6, §13 item 3); institutional completeness including medical services (pp.99–102) is not present. Transcendence as “institutionalized awe, insulated hierarchy, non-participative decisions, tradition as the authority of the eternal yesterday” (pp.128–138) is partly codeable on founder-myth and clergy titles (§13 item 1), not her “eternal yesterday.”

**Q3.** Something expensive is present. Her path is that sacrifice raises the psychic expense of having joined, so members justify staying by belief (p.91). The packet shows cost plus later defense (scarcity; critics as misunderstanding; §7), not that sequence as her measured path. Iannaccone’s price on outside activity: 1992 body still closed.

**Q6.** Confession-like practices are in the ritual table (frames; fear inventory; “turned on” check-in). Mutual criticism, surveillance, and probationary socialization (pp.122–125) are only partly standing (partner-reputation rank; reported doubt-reporting, S2). Do not upgrade every frame to her mortification.

**Q7.** “No goal except to feel” and “OM is OM” are official meaning claims (§1, S6, S8). Founder-myth and rulebook are room authority plus doctrine monopoly — closer to her transcendence pages than a slogan alone, still not tradition-as-yesterday.

**Q8.** Success cut is 25 years of community duration; no unsuccessful coded case exceeded 16 (Appendix p.260). Organizational analogues: OneTaste Inc. 2004/2005–October 2018 US close (§0, S1); Institute of OM / Eros still selling the practice in 2026 (S8–S12). Split corporate/product span — not member-commitment duration.

**Q10.** Stated in advance, a commitment-mechanism reading would be strained if the three problems cannot be separated; if cost exists without her belief-justification path; if media-plus-demo invitation alone produces belonging; if taking an intro and leaving is common and unpenalized beyond money already spent (S13; S2); if the successor keeps the practice after the company name is burned (primitive 17).

### Conversion / network

**Q1.** Entry runs through a mix: friend/referral ties, unknown guests at free evenings and demos, and media-first strangers (§0, §3, §7). Proportion is not quantified.

**Q3.** Progressive revelation: intros sell wellness and “practice not sex”; immersion reveals house rhythm, ladder, and — in reported inner paths — assigned pairings (§3, §7, primitive 18). Trusted or media-warmed attachment, then distinctions — consistent with Stark 1996’s belief-follows-attachment restatement, extended to a secular/wellness syllabus.

**Q4 (partial).** Of the seven 1965 conditions, as the conversion note recovered them: affective bond is present as evening-and-demo warmth (their “cult” names D.P., not this case). Intensive interaction is present in houses and immersions — not daily contact that makes every verbal convert a deployable agent. Tension is an environmental demand in §0b, not their measured “acutely felt, enduring discrepancy.” Religious problem-solving perspective and religious seekership are mismatched (wellness, not church; §0, §1). Turning point is not systematically evidenced. Extra-cult neutralization is contradicted for modal intro. Funnel order cannot be tested. *Doomsday Cult* remains closed.

**Q5 (partial).** Affective bond and intensive interaction — the two that survived Snow and Phillips’ partial critique — are more visible than the predisposing three. Necessity is not shown.

**Q6.** Stark 1996: growth requires internal ties plus an open social network; social implosion stops recruitment. Media and free evenings require outward ties. NDAs, Muggles, and house closure are inward. Open-network at public entry; inward later in house/staff paths.

**Q7.** Mix, weighted more to media than a peer-only invite model. Journalism, TED, and a public site existed; live demo is the in-room closer (§0, §7). Not the 1980-abstract media-occult boundary.

**Q8.** The packet often mixes layers. Media/demo is entry. House, coaching, and certification are retention. Official 35,000 / 16,000 / 1,300 and Huet’s unmatched trio are throughput (§11, S8, S2). Opened conversion bodies explain entry, not maintenance.

### NRM interiors

**Q1.** Harvey’s opened set is written around whole-life entanglement and high exit cost from leaving employer, housing, and social orbit. House/staff participants did co-reside (§6; §13 item 4). What remains without co-residence for a 2026 digital member: jargon, practitioner identity, membership ladder. Exit cost is financial and social for modal intro; higher once the house graph exists (§13 item 10).

**Q2.** Lifton makes milieu control the base condition on which the other seven themes depend (Ch.22). The packet can show NDAs and a public container PDF (§13 item 5; S6). That does not test his dependency claim; applying it here would be asserted.

**Q7 (partial).** Harvey’s two senses, as the NRM note states them: environment-creating and life-claiming. The packet’s split is time-bounded class vs lifestyle (§13 item 4). People took an intro and left. The two senses look separable for modal class buyers.

**Q8 (partial).** Disconfirming “blurred boundaries → high exit cost” would be intensity without Harvey-scale entanglement and without high exit cost. The packet records leave-after-intro paths, with financial/social exit penalties (§13 item 10; S13). That undercuts the strong link for modal intro, not for house/staff (already flagged for counsel).

### Brand community / fandom

**Q1.** Consciousness of kind does not require a competing wellness brand. Practitioner / “turned on” vs Muggle / asleep is the membership mark (§2, §6). Named enemy: numbness, scarcity, later the press (§0b, §12). Muniz and O’Guinn allowed Macintosh consciousness without an opponent as a universal requirement.

**Q2.** Interacting brand community, not a non-interacting brand public. Houses, circles, demos, Connection Program: structured relationships among buyers of the same branded service.

**Q3 (partial).** Their moral responsibility is limited and specialized: integrate and retain; assist in proper use. OneTaste’s enroll/sell/certify path and Connection “Leadership and Service” (§4b, §7) thicken as the ladder is climbed. Not measured; not obviously “limited liability” for those who sold or staffed houses.

**Q4.** Kozinets treats religion/myth talk as a fan legitimacy device against commercial status. Official OneTaste talk does the reverse: attention-training, science-adjacent papers, “not sex” (S6, S8, §11). Stigma-management by wellnessizing. Whether Eros Sutras index more than legitimacy is not decidable from this packet.

**Q6 (partial).** Self-marking: container/form vocabulary, role nouns, coach/teacher (§2). Recruiting: mix — media-primary at the top of the funnel, interaction-primary in rooms. 2026 is more platform-mediated, not a largely-online public (§5, §7, S9).

### Movement failure

**Q1.** OneTaste is not a cycle-of-contention case. Tarrow’s definition (2022, p.195) does not apply. Closed vs transformative is not a fork to run. Public-record sentences and a successor Institute of OM are not that ending. Stop.

**Q2–Q4.** Not this case. Radicalization and institutionalization (pp.201–202) are contention terms. Differential decline of a periphery (pp.202–203) is a cycle-demobilization claim. Early-riser pattern with opportunity held constant (p.196) cannot be posed here.

**Q5.** Neither as a single default. The packet can stand as an ended-organization study of OneTaste Inc. (US close, October 2018; S1, §0, §12) *and* as a continuing-practice study of Institute of OM / Eros (2026 memberships, coaching, Connection Program; S8–S12; primitive 17). It is not “one ended scene.” It is still not Anderson’s scene. It is still not Tarrow’s cycle. Do not collapse the three. Anderson 2009 *Sociological Forum* was opened 2026-08-20 (Wiley). Her sample is the Philadelphia rave/EDM scene. Her five named forces — generational schism, commercialization, cultural otherness/deviance and self-destruction, formal social control, genre-based scene fragmentation — are her claims, not this case.

**Q6 (limited).** Fillieule’s plurality: individual disengagement versus collective decline / field collapse (2015, opening pages). Huet’s apostates and the 2018 refund spike are individual disengagement (§4, S2, S13). OneTaste Inc. US ops dissolved. Successor practice continues. Field collapse is not observed.

## 3. Questions left unanswered

**Commitment.** Q4: substitution and screening — later Iannaccone-channel labels; 1992 body closed. Q5: club good with participatory crowding vs private good merely co-consumed — Iannaccone body closed; official product is individual connection (§1). Co-consumption is visible; his crowding-quality claim is not licensed. Q9: Hall 1988 still closed. Media-plus-demo invitation could account for first belonging without the six-mechanism story.

**Conversion / network.** Q2: overbalance cannot be operationalized — secondary wording, not an opened 1980 AJS body; no member-tie / nonmember-tie ratio at joining. Q4 remainder: religious three, turning point, extra-cult neutralization for modal intro, funnel order. *Doomsday Cult* closed. Q5 necessity: no comparison sample.

**NRM interiors.** Q3: stayers and leavers exist, but not two populations under roughly constant intensity from the same period. Q4: cannot sample retained and departed from the same period as a design. Q5: mode of exit vs age of socialisation — neither systematically evidenced. Q6: quota and hour counts are organizational metrics (§11, S2), not Ransom et al.’s self-measurement of the self. Lalich and Introvigne 2022 stay closed.

**Brand community / fandom.** Q5: internal heterogeneity vs continuity — unanswered. Thomas, Price and Schau 2013 stays closed; house vs digital vs Connection Program are era/product lanes, not Kozinets’ elective affinities. Schouten and McAlexander 1995 stays closed.

**Movement failure.** Q1–Q4 as Tarrow case tests: mismatch, not “unanswered pending more OneTaste facts.” Taylor 1989, Tarrow 2011 Part III, and Tarrow 2022 Chs 9–11 remain unopened. Anderson 2009 is opened and not imported.
