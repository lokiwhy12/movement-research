Purpose: First-pass movement-DNA extraction packet for the LDS / Mormon missionary and retention system centered on The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. This is an analytical record of conversion, covenant-path progression, and community-retention mechanics — not an endorsement or a theological critique.

## Executive summary
The LDS system is one of the clearest large-scale examples of durable religious movement design in the modern world. Its power does not rest on a single recruitment tactic; it comes from an integrated pipeline that connects missionary labor, family transmission, congregational embedding, structured doctrine, volunteer callings, temple progression, daily/weekly study routines, and a strong life-stage architecture. A convert is not merely persuaded of a set of beliefs. They are invited into a complete social, moral, and temporal structure.

Movement-DNA read: the LDS system combines **high-discipline missionary outreach**, **scripture-and-restoration truth claims**, **rapid congregational embedding**, **covenant-path progression**, **family-centered repetition**, **dense volunteer-role assignment**, **ward/stake social infrastructure**, and **temple-oriented long-term ascent**. Its unusual strength is that conversion is quickly tied to durable routines and community roles, so identity is reinforced by both doctrine and repeated embodied participation.

## 0b) Environmental conditions ("why it worked")
- **Strong restoration claim:** the church offers not just inspiration but the assertion of restored authority, scripture, and ordinances.
- **Life-structure hunger:** members and converts receive a complete weekly and lifetime framework, not just occasional motivation.
- **Family and community density:** belonging is reinforced through households, local wards, callings, youth structures, and intergenerational continuity.
- **Missionary scale:** large numbers of volunteer missionaries create constant outward-facing recruitment capacity.
- **Covenant progression logic:** the path does not stop at baptism; it extends through ordinances, temple worship, service, and family history.
- **Volunteer ownership:** unpaid local leadership and callings make members co-producers of the religious system.

## 1) Promise + positioning (the offer)
- **Explicit promise:** come unto Christ through His restored gospel, receive ordinances and covenants, and walk the covenant path.
- **Implicit promise:** your life can be placed inside a divinely ordered structure with truth, belonging, purpose, and eternal continuity.
- **Pain named:** spiritual confusion, lack of purpose, fragmented family life, moral drift, isolation from covenant community.
- **Before/after identity:** from seeker/investigator/outsider -> baptized member on the covenant path with an active role in a ward community.
- **Time-to-win framing:** meaningful participation starts immediately through lessons, church attendance, prayer/scripture study, and then baptism/confirmation.

### Why the offer converts
1. **It claims authority, not just advice** — restoration and priesthood claims make the invitation stronger than generic spirituality.
2. **It provides a complete social world** — meetings, callings, ministering, study, service, and family practices all interlock.
3. **It gives rapid belonging** — investigators and new members are quickly invited into church, home, and community surfaces.
4. **It makes progression visible** — baptism, confirmation, sacrament participation, callings, temple recommend, and temple ordinances provide an ascent ladder.

## 2) Worldview primitives / glossary
1. **Restoration** — core claim that Christ’s original church, authority, and ordinances have been restored in modern times.
2. **Book of Mormon** — central additional scripture and a major proof-text in missionary teaching.
3. **Preach My Gospel** — official missionary guide structuring teaching, goals, planning, and coordination.
4. **Missionary purpose** — the formal purpose language shaping missionary identity and work.
5. **Teaching missionary** — full-time missionary assigned away from home to teach the gospel.
6. **Service missionary** — missionary serving locally in church/community assignments while living at home.
7. **Investigator / person being taught** — prospective convert engaged in lessons with missionaries.
8. **Invitation to be baptized** — formal conversion milestone built directly into missionary teaching.
9. **Baptism** — first major ordinance of formal entry into church membership.
10. **Confirmation** — post-baptism ordinance tied to receiving the Holy Ghost and full membership integration.
11. **Covenant path** — official progression frame for moving deeper through ordinances and commitments.
12. **Sacrament meeting** — central weekly congregational worship surface and major belonging checkpoint.
13. **Ward** — local congregation and primary day-to-day social unit.
14. **Stake** — regional grouping of wards and a secondary leadership/community layer.
15. **Bishop / bishopric** — local lay leadership with strong gatekeeping and care functions.
16. **Ward mission leader** — local coordinator for missionary efforts and weekly coordination.
17. **Weekly missionary coordination meeting** — recurring planning surface aligning members and missionaries around teaching, new members, and returning members.
18. **Ministering** — structured care-and-contact system that replaced home/visiting teaching.
19. **Calling** — volunteer church assignment that converts members from attendees into contributors.
20. **Temple recommend** — worthiness/access credential for temple participation.
21. **Temple** — sacred ordinance space and high-status progression surface beyond weekly meetings.
22. **Ordinances** — formal sacred rites such as baptism, confirmation, and temple ordinances.
23. **Worthiness** — moral/behavioral qualification logic used for service and temple access.
24. **Priesthood** — authority/governance concept structuring male ordination and much of institutional function.
25. **Relief Society** — major women’s organization integrated into congregational life and ministering.
26. **Elders quorum** — male organizational unit tied to service, ministering, and local governance.
27. **Come, Follow Me** — official church study manual and recurring home/church scripture routine.
28. **Family home / home-centered study** — domestic religious reinforcement layer complementing meetings.
29. **Seminary / institute** — age-targeted educational reinforcement for youth and young adults.
30. **Temple and family history work** — genealogical/ordinance lane that deepens intergenerational identity.
31. **New and returning members** — official category for structured retention and reintegration effort.
32. **Love, share, invite** — official simplified member-missionary grammar for outreach behavior.
33. **Mission call** — authoritative assignment process that formalizes missionary service.
34. **Set apart** — ritual authorization into a calling or missionary assignment.
35. **Gather Israel** — large-frame mission language that turns outreach into sacred world-historical work.

### Working grammar
- **restoration + scripture + authority** = truth-claim legitimacy
- **missionaries + lessons + baptism invitation** = formal conversion pipeline
- **ward + sacrament meeting + friendship** = early belonging
- **callings + ministering + study routines** = retention through repeated role participation
- **covenant path + temple + family history** = long-term ascension and identity deepening

## 3) Representative transformation sequence
1. **Relational contact / finding**  
   - Goal: identify a person willing to hear the message.  
   - Mechanism: member invitation, missionary outreach, events, lessons, local social contact.

2. **Restoration teaching sequence**  
   - Goal: establish truth-claim seriousness and doctrinal coherence.  
   - Mechanism: missionary lessons, Book of Mormon use, prayer, testimony, restoration narrative.

3. **Invitation and experiment**  
   - Goal: move the prospect from abstract curiosity to enacted practice.  
   - Mechanism: prayer, scripture reading, church attendance, meeting missionaries, direct invitation to baptism.

4. **Congregational embedding**  
   - Goal: ensure the person encounters a living social body, not just missionaries.  
   - Mechanism: sacrament meeting, ward activities, home invitations, local member support.

5. **Baptism and confirmation**  
   - Goal: formalize entry through ordinances and covenant identity.  
   - Mechanism: baptismal service, confirmation, public recognition, new-member support.

6. **Retention through structure**  
   - Goal: prevent drift after initial conversion.  
   - Mechanism: ministering, friendship, service opportunities, study habits, weekly coordination, covenant-path tracking.

7. **Role acquisition and routine deepening**  
   - Goal: transform the convert from recipient into participant.  
   - Mechanism: callings, class participation, family history, seminary/institute, quorum/Relief Society integration.

8. **Temple progression and intergenerational lock-in**  
   - Goal: connect present membership to long-term sacred ascent and family continuity.  
   - Mechanism: temple recommend process, temple ordinances, family history work, covenant-path language.

### 4. Life-stage continuity

- **Mechanisms:** baptism, confirmation, temple recommend, temple ordinances, family history, returning-member support.
- **Why it pulls:** there is always a meaningful next step, framed as sacred progression rather than mere status climbing.

## 7) Distinctive mechanics worth preserving in the corpus
- **Missionary front-end + congregational back-end integration** — outreach is tightly connected to local retention structures.
- **Callings as anti-passivity design** — volunteer roles convert consumers of religion into operators of the system.
- **Covenant path as visible ascension logic** — a powerful example of long-term progression framing.
- **Household plus congregation dual-loop** — home study and church meetings reinforce each other instead of competing.
- **Intergenerational continuity via temple/family history** — membership extends backward and forward through family lines.

## 8) Shadow risk / caution signal
The LDS system is a caution case because dense structure and sincere care can also create conformity pressure, authority dependence, and difficulty for marginal or doubting members. Risks include over-identification of worth with participation and worthiness metrics, social costs for drifting away, gendered/authority asymmetries, and strong internal-external boundary maintenance. Highly integrated systems can deliver real belonging while also making dissent, ambiguity, or exit more socially expensive.

## 9) Safe translation note
Reusable structural lessons:
- integrate recruitment with real local belonging rather than leaving converts alone after the initial decision,
- create recurring low-cost routines that keep identity alive between major milestones,
- assign meaningful roles early so members become builders, not spectators,
- use a visible long-term path that gives people an intelligible next step,
- reinforce identity through both household practice and community structure.

Non-reusable / high-risk elements:
- heavy authority claims without healthy review mechanisms,
- moral worth scoring that collapses personhood into compliance,
- social systems that make exit or doubt unusually costly,
- over-structured identity that leaves little room for experimentation,
- volunteer extraction without attention to burnout or autonomy.

Safe systems need opt-in agency, transparent boundaries, softer status gradients, and space for belonging without high-pressure conformity.

## Source-anchor excerpts / evidence notes
- **General Handbook 23:** the church explicitly frames sharing the gospel and strengthening new and returning members as linked work, not separate functions, and recommends weekly coordination meetings focused on those being taught, recent converts, and returning members.
- **General Handbook 23:** official member grammar is simplified to “love, share, invite,” and the handbook explicitly encourages invitations into sacrament meeting, ward activities, homes, church programs, and lessons with missionaries.
- **General Handbook 23:** new members are described as needing friendship, opportunities to serve, and spiritual nourishment, which is a concise statement of the retention architecture.
- **General Handbook 24:** missionary service is treated as sacred privilege and, for worthy able young men, a priesthood responsibility; the handbook also differentiates teaching, service, and senior missionary assignments.
- **Preach My Gospel (2023) table of contents:** the missionary system is highly structured around finding, teaching, inviting to baptism, helping people prepare for baptism and confirmation, making commitments, and uniting with local leaders to establish the church.
- **Come, Follow Me page:** official study manuals are explicitly positioned as resources for personal and family scripture study, confirming that doctrinal repetition is not limited to Sunday meetings.
- **Ministering FAQ:** ministering replaced home and visiting teaching and reoriented local care toward more direct Christlike watching over and service, showing the church’s effort to keep relational oversight active.
- **Wikipedia:** the church’s scale, global missionary force, hierarchical ward/stake structure, temple system, and open canon show how institutional breadth supports continuity and expansion.
