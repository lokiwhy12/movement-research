## Executive summary

Occupy Wall Street was a 59-day encampment in Zuccotti Park (Liberty Square) in Lower Manhattan, September 17 to November 15, 2011, that became a portable name, a slogan, and a decision process. Wikipedia (pointer-then-open): left-wing populist / progressive movement against economic inequality, corporate influence, and the aftermath of the 2008 crash and *Citizens United*; forced out of the park on November 15; then turned toward banks, campuses, foreclosed homes, and social media. The Canadian magazine Adbusters issued the July 13, 2011 call. OccupyWallSt.org (Justine Tunney, July 14, 2011 per the About page) became the oldest online hub. Roughly 200 New York activists met at Tompkins Square Park and on the september17 list before day one. David Graeber is credited on Wikipedia with an early role and with helping coin "We are the 99%"; that attribution is Wikipedia-mediated, not a Graeber-book open.

Movement-DNA read from pages opened this pass: the adhesion engine was not a demand sheet and not a founder. It was a three-layer stack — (1) a one-sentence identity ("we are the 99%") that anyone in debt, foreclosure, or a bad job could put on a sign; (2) a physical container (the park as a 24-hour city-within-a-city: kitchen, library, medical, sanitation, GA); (3) a process religion (general assembly, modified consensus, human microphone, progressive stack, working groups) that made showing up feel like becoming the government. The Adbusters call wanted one demand and 20,000 bodies. The NYCGA that formed refused one demand, renamed the park Liberty Square, and treated process as the product. That refusal was inclusion. It was also the decay seed: once the park was gone, the ritual that produced belonging had no room, and the money that had arrived because of the park became an argument.

Layer split (do not collapse):

- Adbusters voice (opened, July 13, 2011 archive): a "shift in revolutionary tactics"; Tahrir fused with Spanish acampadas; swarm not wolf-pack; seize a symbolic square; repeat one demand; candidate demand = a presidential commission to end money's influence in Washington; "democracy not corporatocracy"; 20,000 people, tents, kitchens, "peaceful barricades," a few months. Paraphrased. Not a script.
- NYCGA / occupywallst.org voice (opened): people-powered movement from September 17, 2011; 100-plus U.S. cities and 1,500-plus global actions (About); fighting banks and the 1%; inspired by Egypt and Tunisia; Declaration of the Occupation (Sept. 29, 2011); Principles of Solidarity (consensus Sept. 23, 2011; open-access amendment Feb. 9, 2012); Statement of Autonomy (Nov. 10, 2011; revised Mar. 3, 2012) — party-less, leaderless, not a brand, not for sale, speak with us not for us; Community Agreement / Safer Spaces (Feb. 20, 2012); Spokes Council (early Nov. 2011) as an operational patch; later About voice: "The Occupy movement was crushed in 2012."
- External reporting / scholarship (opened): Wikipedia Occupy Wall Street; CNN and Marketplace on the ~$800,000 donation pile and the January 2012 freeze; Observer Oct. 18, 2011 balance sheet; Village Voice $100,000 bail fund; NY Post / BBC pizza dollars and "stop sending pizza"; Graeber Guardian May 7, 2012 (liberal influx, $0.5m-plus, evictions, May Day alliance); Gitlin via The Rumpus (three-week conversation shift vs three-year sixties; demandlessness as inclusion; outsider identity vs bandwagon). New Yorker Schwartz and Vanity Fair oral history were thin this pass — not reconstructed.

This is a failed-control movement: fascination (meme + camp + process) without a durable container, a durable money grammar, or a durable role ladder after eviction. Mechanics extract. Official voice and external documentation stay labeled. Needs counsel review. No legal conclusions.

## Extracted primitives (portable, analytical)

Numbered 1–18. These are the output-template primitives. Observational. Not a build spec. Not instructions.

1. Identity compression — "we are the 99%." A statistical claim became a first-person plural. Principles and occupywallst.org About treat it as the name of the we. Wikipedia: Tumblr variation plus a CBO inequality report as booster. Portable because it required no membership card.
2. Leaderless as doctrine — Autonomy: party-less, leaderless, not a business, not a brand, not for sale. occupywallst.org About simultaneously names Adbusters (Lasn / White) as call authors and ~200 Tompkins Square people as "founders." The contradiction is the mechanic: charisma was distributed, then denied.
3. Process-as-product — the General Assembly was the offer. Declaration close: occupy space, create a process, generate solutions accessible to everyone. Showing up *was* the transformation.
4. Modified consensus — Wikipedia: attempt consensus, then 9/10 if needed. Block / stand-aside as moral weight. Inclusion that could also stall. Observer: finance decisions over $100 had to pass the GA until the spokes patch.
5. Human microphone / people's mic — Wikipedia: no amplified-sound permit; speaker pauses; nearby crowd repeats; NYCGA.cc Oct. 2: "two generations of people's mic will be required." Synchrony without a PA. Cost: time, voice, weather.
6. Encampment as proof — the body stayed overnight. Wikipedia: 100–200 sleepers; tents initially disallowed; sleeping bags / blankets; kitchen ~$1,000/day. The camp was the argument that another world was already being lived.
7. Meme-first launch — Adbusters poster (dancer on the Charging Bull) plus #OCCUPYWALLSTREET. Wikipedia: Lasn registered OccupyWallStreet.org June 9; Tunney registered OccupyWallSt.org in July; Facebook listed 125 Occupy-related pages by mid-October. The name copied before the process did.
8. Working groups as a parallel city — Comfort, Medical, Legal, Accounting, Direct Action, Arts and Culture, Safer Spaces, People's Library, kitchen, livestream. NYCGA.cc: working groups are autonomous; welcome table and info table as intake. Vanity Fair intro (thin): field hospital, library, sanitation, free-cigarette dispensary.
9. Progressive stack — Wikipedia: marginalized speakers sometimes moved forward; white men waited; "step forward, or step back." A speaking-order ethic as belonging proof for some and as a status inversion for others.
10. Demandlessness as inclusion — Adbusters wanted one demand. NYCGA issued grievances "not all-inclusive" and deferred demands. Wikipedia: a 99 Percent Declaration group was rejected as a co-opt. Gitlin-via-Rumpus: concrete demands can undermine a young movement and bestow authority on a system many did not endorse.
11. In-kind logistics as the real budget — pizza from Germany / New Zealand (BBC / NY Post); produce from upstate; Ben & Jerry's scoops; UFT storage; COBA food for ~800 (NBC not opened; CNN names the in-kind pattern). Cash was secondary until it wasn't.
12. Anti-money movement meets a pile of money — Observer Oct. 18: $454,437.47 in / $55,131.85 out (13%). CNN / Marketplace: ~$800,000 trackable across the year; January freeze; leftover earmarked for bail/legal. Marketplace: $1,400 tea, $4,200 T-shirts as fight-starters. The money forced a grammar the process was not built to run.
13. Spokes council as efficiency patch — occupywallst.org Nov. 3, 2011: Structure Working Group; coordinate operations and budgets; unburden the GA from uncoordinated funding requests; spoke recallable at any time. The horizontal claim met scale and invented a fluid representative.
14. Rapid franchise — occupywallst.org About: 100-plus U.S. cities, 1,500-plus global. Wikipedia: sister libraries; Occupy Sandy reused NYCGA.net a year later. The copyable unit was the name + a GA, not a charter.
15. Eviction as identity rupture — Wikipedia: shortly after midnight Nov. 15, 2011; owner notice; ~200 arrests; return without sleeping bags, tarps, tents. CNN: loss of the park = loss of momentum, media, and in-kind food; housing then cost $2,700/two weeks in churches. The ritual room was the product. Removing it removed the product.
16. Offshoot persistence without the camp — Wikipedia: Occupy Sandy; Strike Debt (~$4 million student loans / 2,761 Everest students — Wikipedia-mediated); Occupy the SEC (325-page Volcker letter — named, body not opened); Alternative Banking Group. The DNA that traveled was mutual-aid and comment-letter skill, not the 24-hour square.
17. Brand-use afterlife — Autonomy: not a brand, not for sale; statements not on nycga.net are independent. occupywallst.org About (later): do not trust leftover social-media accounts; @OccupyWallStNYC lawsuit named. OpenSecrets 2023–24: Occupy Democrats Election Fund, a Carey/hybrid PAC, $3,422,620 raised / $3,177,486 spent / $106,600 to Democratic candidates. Later occupy.org is a Jason Samel / Occupy This Album site, not NYCGA. The name outlived the assembly.
18. Decay signature — fascination (meme + camp + process) without a durable room, a durable money grammar, or a durable role after eviction. Gitlin-via-Rumpus: success meant quandaries; mainstream wanted a bandwagon; outsider identity thinned. Wikipedia criticism layer: no legislative victories; too tied to the park; faded after Nov. 15. Official later voice: crushed in 2012.

## 0b) Environmental conditions (why now / why it scaled)

3–7 condition → demand → promise:

1. Post-2008 foreclosure, unemployment, and TARP-visible bailouts → demand that someone name the banks as the problem and stay in public until the name stuck → movement: we are the 99%; they took houses and bailouts (Declaration, paraphrased).
2. *Citizens United* (Jan. 2010) and a sense that elections were bought → demand that money be separated from representation → Adbusters candidate demand: a presidential commission to end money's influence; "democracy not corporatocracy."
3. Arab Spring / Tahrir and Spanish acampadas as a newly available picture of a square that governs itself → demand for a local square that felt like that picture → Adbusters: Tahrir fused with acampadas; seize a symbolic square.
4. Social isolation plus social media (Twitter, Tumblr, livestream, Facebook pages) → demand to be physically next to people who felt the same debt and rage → park as Liberty Square; 125 Occupy-related Facebook pages by mid-October (Wikipedia).
5. Institutional distrust (Congress, banks, both parties) → demand for a we that was not a party → Autonomy: party-less, leaderless, not for sale; speak with us, not for us.
6. A generation whose rebellion had gone sarcastic (Gitlin-via-Rumpus) → demand for earnest public seriousness → Gitlin: sweetness and earnestness; three-week conversation shift vs three-year sixties.
7. After Nov. 15, no 24-hour room → demand that the feeling survive without the park → it mostly did not as a camp; it persisted as slogan, offshoot, and later brand-use.

Meaning crisis named (movement, implicit and explicit): ordinary political life is a mortgaged future; democracy is not democracy when economic power writes the rules; you are not crazy for being in debt; you are the 99%.

Enemy / chaos named:
- Movement: Wall Street / the 1% / corporate forces that put profit over people (Declaration; About). Adbusters: financial Gomorrah; corporatocracy.
- Autonomy's other enemy: people who capitalize on the movement or appropriate its symbols.
- External: the protesters themselves as nuisance / class warfare (Romney, Rasmussen — Wikipedia); the police; the park owner. Official 2011 pages opened this pass do not name Bloomberg as the metaphysical enemy; they name corporations.

Certainty / belonging / status offered: a first-person plural that required no résumé; a nightly assembly where a walk-in could speak; a kitchen that did not charge; a library; a role (Comfort, Legal, stack-keeper) available the same week. After eviction: a slogan without a room.

## 1) Promise + positioning (the offer)

Explicit promise (1 sentence, NYCGA / Declaration): we have peaceably assembled to let these facts be known — corporations run governments — and we urge you to occupy space, create a process, and generate solutions accessible to everyone.

Explicit promise (1 sentence, Adbusters, paraphrased): flood lower Manhattan on September 17, stay for months, and repeat one demand until the government must choose the people over corporate lucre; leading candidate demand = a presidential commission to end money in politics.

Explicit promise (1 sentence, occupywallst.org About): a people-powered movement fighting the corrosive power of major banks and the richest 1% over an unfair global economy that is foreclosing on the future.

Implicit promise: if you come and stay, you are already the 99%, already the government, already living the alternative. You do not have to win a bill to have won the day. Continuation (another night, another city, another working group) is how the feeling stays true.

Pain named: foreclosure; bailouts plus bonuses; student debt; workplace discrimination; outsourced labor; sold privacy; captured media and courts (Declaration grievance themes, paraphrased — full list not dumped).

Time-to-win: Adbusters: a few months of 20,000-strong presence. Actual camp: 59 days. Conversation win (Gitlin-via-Rumpus): weeks, not years. Legislative win: not claimed as delivered on pages opened.

Before / after identity: isolated debtor / unemployed / disgusted citizen → occupier / the 99% / autonomous political being inside Liberty Square.

Positioning markers (opened): leaderless; no one demand; human mic; GA; 99%; copyable city name.

### Working score
- Clarity: 5/5 on the identity sentence; 2/5 on what winning looks like. The system chose identity over a bill.
- Believability: 4/5 in 2011 (the park was real; the kitchen was real; inequality numbers were public). 2/5 as a durable government-replacement after the park closed.
- Urgency: 5/5 in Sept.–Oct. 2011 (crash memory, arrests, livestream). 2/5 after winter 2011–12.
- Distinctiveness: 5/5 vs ordinary protest (camp + process + meme). The signature was staying, not marching once.

## 3) Transformation sequence (the protocol)

Representative arc from pages opened this pass. Not a universal occupier biography. Not a how-to.

1. Aspirational contact — Adbusters poster / tweet; a friend's livestream; a union march. Goal: ordinary politics is spiritually insufficient. Expected state: recognition, anger.
2. Low-friction yes — walk into the park; stand at a GA; eat from the kitchen (NY Post / BBC / Wikipedia: food was free). Goal: body in the room.
3. Threshold night — sleep in the park (sleeping bag / blanket; tents later contested). Goal: you are an occupier. Expected state: belonging, discomfort, weather.
4. Language install — 99%; mic check; twinkle; stack. Goal: you can speak the we.
5. Working-group yes — Comfort, Legal, Media, Accounting, Safer Spaces. Goal: a role, not just a sign.
6. Process immersion — nightly GA; two-generation people's mic; consensus on $100-plus spends. Goal: you are the government. Expected state: exaltation or exhaustion.
7. Escalation — facilitation; spoke; livestream; arrest and bail (Village Voice grammar); travel to another occupation.
8. Stress — money fights; sanitation / assault allegations (Wikipedia); owner cleaning threat (Oct. 13); eviction (Nov. 15).
9. Afterlife — church floor; May Day; Occupy Sandy; Strike Debt; or walk away with the slogan. No official shunning doctrine on pages opened.

### Working score
- Repeatability: 5/5 for the name-plus-GA franchise; 2/5 for the 24-hour Zuccotti container (privately owned park + no-amp rule + weather).
- Speed: 5/5 to a felt win (same-day kitchen, same-night voice). 1/5 to a legislative win.
- Durability: 2/5 as a camp-based identity. 4/5 as a slogan that still names inequality.

## 4) Emotional arc (attention to catharsis to resolve)

Typical 2011 join (inferred from Declaration, About, Wikipedia camp, Graeber, Gitlin-via-Rumpus, pizza/donation reporting):

1. Recognition — the 99% sentence fits a private debt story.
2. Arrival shock — drums, kitchen, library, cops at the edge (BBC early: small numbers, rain, sleepers through the march).
3. Voice — first people's-mic repetition; first stack.
4. Belonging drip — free food; a working-group shift; someone knows your name.
5. Peak-state nights — mass arrests as proof the we is real (Wikipedia: 768 Brooklyn Bridge, Oct. 1); celebrity drop-ins; Time's "The Protester."
6. Dissonance — whose money; whose drum; whose assault allegation; is this the 99% or a scene.
7. Catharsis or rupture — eviction; January freeze; "crushed in 2012." Offshoot resolve (Sandy, Strike Debt) is a different product.

Biggest I-get-it-now shifts (opened): first night in the park; first time the crowd repeats your sentence; first time the kitchen feeds you without a transaction; first time the GA argues for hours about $100. External I-get-it-now is sometimes the eviction video or the leftover PAC — contested as meaning.

### Working score
- Intensity control: 2/5 — the camp had no published intensity cap; drums, weather, arrest, and process length ran hot. Safer Spaces / Community Agreement (Feb. 2012) is a later attempt to name consent, substances, and removal — after the main occupation.
- Coherence: 4/5 inside the 99% sentence; 2/5 across demands, tactics, and money.

## 4b) Psychological pull factors

Top drivers (mechanism → moment → reinforcement):

1. Instant we — a slogan that required no résumé → I am already inside → stay another night.
2. Body proof — sleeping, cooking, assembling in the same square → the alternative is not a pamphlet → return.
3. Voice amplification — human mic and progressive stack → I was heard in a chorus → identity fusion.
4. Role without a boss — working groups "are autonomous" (NYCGA.cc) → competence and status without a title → escalate into facilitation or finance.
5. Moral emergency plus variable reward — arrest livestreams, celebrity visits, pizza from another country → the world is watching → donate / stay.
6. Process as meaning — hours of consensus → I am not a spectator of democracy → sunk-cost belonging.

Bias stack (activated): commitment/consistency (nights slept, working-group shifts, bail risk); social proof (100-plus cities; 15,000-plus Oct. 5 march — Wikipedia); authority inverted (no leaders, but facilitators / finance / livestream as informal clergy); identity fusion (99% / occupier); cognitive dissonance plus sunk cost (cold, arrest, process hours); scarcity (the park could be cleaned / evicted — Oct. 13 threat); emotional contagion (drums, mic, marches); in-group/out-group (99 vs 1; occupiers vs "they"); meaning/destiny (mortgaged future; another world); reciprocity (free food, free library, free cigarettes per VF intro); liking/unity (welcome table).

### Working score
- Stickiness: 5/5 historically inside the park; 2/5 after eviction.
- Dignity (pull without coercion): 3/5 — official copy is opt-in, anonymous donation, welcome dissent (Autonomy). Process exhaustion, progressive-stack inversion, Safer Spaces removal clauses, and camp safety allegations sit next to that. Not a legal conclusion.
