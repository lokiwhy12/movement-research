Top 6 movement primitives (draft — expanded with executive claims & implementation examples)

1) Compact doctrine
- What: Constrain the movement's public-facing beliefs into a short, repeatable "compact" that orients members and lowers friction for onboarding.
- Executive claim: A single clear compact reduces decision friction and accelerates first-week retention.
- Example: Ship a one-page "Core Commitments" card used during onboarding and linked from the signup flow.

2) Pull-factor (onboarding architecture)
- What: Design onboarding hooks that generate immediate perceived value and social proof.
- Executive claim: A designed quick-win on day 0 increases activation by making value obvious within 24 hours.
- Example: 24-hour starter ritual + community shoutout that grants a visible "starter" badge.

3) Ritual map (habit loop)
- What: Sequence of actions, signals, and rewards that keep participants returning without coercion.
- Executive claim: A predictable micro-ritual cadence drives weekly engagement without raising commitment thresholds.
- Example: Three-step daily check-in (prompt → micro-action → measurable feedback) delivered by email/app at a fixed time.

4) Felt-alignment / emotional climate
- What: Engineering emotional temperature (soothing vs vigilance) to sustain retention.
- Executive claim: Prioritize warmth-first interactions during onboarding; escalate intensity only after demonstrated commitment.
- Example: Welcome sequence with warm-tone mentors, then optional high-intensity challenges after week 2.

5) Institution-bound container vs portability
- What: Tradeoffs between tightly controlled branded containers and portable participant assets.
- Executive claim: Combine a strong branded container for culture with transferable participant artifacts to reduce long-term churn.
- Example: Provide branded cohorts plus downloadable "transferable playbooks" that learners keep as their credential.

6) Transparency & room legibility
- What: Publicly legible signals vs private operator-mediated rituals.
- Executive claim: Visible governance and boundary rules lower perceived risk and broaden mainstream adoption.
- Example: Publish a short governance rubric and a public FAQ describing which rituals are private vs public.
