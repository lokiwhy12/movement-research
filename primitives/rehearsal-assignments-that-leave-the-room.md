# Rehearsal + assignments that leave the room

Definition
--
Rehearsal + assignments that leave the room are mechanics where participants do not just understand the frame inside the container — they are quickly asked to perform it somewhere else. The room becomes a launchpad: make the call, tell the truth, share the possibility, invite the friend, do tomorrow's workout, log the proof, repeat the distinction in ordinary life. The core function is transfer from high-intensity experience into offstage behavior.

One-paragraph example
--
Landmark shows the clearest interpersonal form of this primitive. The participant is not left with a private insight; the system pushes that insight into immediate behavior outside the room through completion calls, possibility-sharing, and Tuesday-evening invites. 75 HARD runs the same transfer pattern in a colder, self-administered format: the motivational moment only counts if it survives into the next day's repeated obligations — two workouts, reading, diet adherence, and proof capture. Different emotional texture, same structural move: the method sticks when the container exports behavior instead of merely producing a feeling.

Why it matters
--
- Converts insight into evidence-bearing behavior quickly.
- Prevents the flagship experience from collapsing into self-contained theater.
- Creates measurable propagation because the practice now lives outside the room.
- Reinforces identity between formal events by making the person act differently in ordinary life.
- Turns members into carriers of the method, not just consumers of the experience.

Shadow risk
--
This primitive becomes dangerous when assignments stop being bounded practice and start becoming compliance theater. The failure modes are pressured confession, coerced reconciliation, unpaid recruitment labor, unsafe physical demands, or emotionally loaded actions framed as proof of integrity. Risk climbs fast when refusal is moralized, when time pressure blocks reflection, or when the system uses outside-the-room action to collapse normal consent boundaries.

Safe translation
--
If a product or community uses off-room assignments, keep the member's agency intact. Use bounded, low-risk behaviors; make timing and scope adjustable; provide opt-outs; and avoid forcing high-stakes family, money, trauma, or health actions just to demonstrate buy-in. The right use of this primitive is transfer into lived practice, not social-pressure theater.

Small-risk-mitigation checklist
--
- Keep assignments reversible, low-stakes, and specific.
- Never require high-conflict conversations on an emotional deadline.
- Do not tie compliance to worth, rank, or moral legitimacy.
- Separate practice assignments from recruiting obligations.
- Preserve a real opt-out path without punishment or shaming.
- Use evidence capture for self-honesty, not surveillance theater.
