# Attraction-led self-selection / engineered recruitment drive

Definition
--
Attraction-led self-selection / engineered recruitment drive is the mechanic where a movement decides how aggressively it tries to acquire new people. In the attraction-led form, growth depends mainly on visible proof, self-recognition, and low-pressure discoverability: the prospect sees something compelling, recognizes themselves in it, and opts in without being chased. In the engineered-recruitment form, growth is supported by explicit acquisition machinery: tests, intro offers, staffed front doors, events, missions, prestige networks, scripts, or dissemination systems designed to move strangers into the funnel. Same growth problem, different recruitment posture. Attraction-led systems trust witness and readiness to do most of the work. Engineered-recruitment systems trust process, outreach structure, and guided conversion architecture.

One-paragraph example
--
AA leans toward attraction-led self-selection. Its official posture is that it does not recruit, only the individual can decide if they are alcoholic, and the strongest proof is ordinary recovered people telling the truth in meetings and sponsor relationships. Scientology leans toward engineered recruitment drive. Its packet describes personality-test style front doors, missions, introductory services, dissemination activity, celebrity prestige overlays, and structured paths from first exposure into recurring auditing and training. Same growth challenge, opposite acquisition answer. AA says: make the room legible, let testimony do the work, and let the person opt in when they recognize themselves. Scientology says: build organized intake lanes, guide the person through entry experiences, and keep recruitment tied to a managed progression architecture.

Why it matters
--
- Determines whether the newcomer feels discovered by the system or processed by it.
- Changes reactance levels: self-selection tends to lower defensive resistance, while engineered recruitment can accelerate conversion but raises the risk of pressure, oversell, and manipulation.
- Shapes what kind of proof does the work: ordinary member witness and ambient reputation versus formal intro offers, outreach staff, events, and prestige signaling.
- Affects scale strategy, because attraction-led systems often spread slower but with stronger voluntary fit, while engineered recruitment systems can scale acquisition faster through deliberate funnel design.
- Reveals whether the movement's growth ethic is "be credible and available" or "actively move people through the front door."

Shadow risk
--
This primitive can fail in both directions. Attraction-led self-selection can become so passive that people who genuinely need the room never cross the threshold, or so allergic to outreach that the movement mistakes obscurity for integrity. Engineered recruitment drive fails harder: the front door can become a pressure funnel where qualification tools, prestige cues, or staged wins are used to move people faster than their informed judgment. The healthy question is whether the acquisition posture helps the right people recognize fit or whether it is optimizing conversion regardless of fit.

Safe translation
--
If a product, community, or movement borrows this mechanic, prefer legible proof, open access, and honest invitations over high-pressure intake funnels. Make the front door clear, show real outcomes, and let people opt in from recognition rather than from engineered urgency. Outreach is fine when it increases access, but it should never outrun informed choice. The useful pattern is visible credibility plus voluntary entry. The dangerous pattern is acquisition architecture that becomes more optimized than the transformation itself.

Small-risk-mitigation checklist
--
- Keep the front door legible: tell people what the offer is, what happens next, and what deeper involvement would actually require.
- Let visible proof do more work than urgency tactics. If conversion depends on pressure, scarcity theater, or emotional oversteer, the intake architecture is outrunning fit.
- Separate access from capture: intro events, tests, or starter products should inform and orient, not quietly commit people to a deeper funnel.
- Avoid moralizing recruitment. When spreading the mission becomes proof of worth, members can start optimizing conversion over consent.
- Audit prestige and social-proof levers so they do not become borrowed authority that substitutes for real outcomes.
- Preserve easy refusal and easy exit at the front door. A healthy acquisition posture can tolerate a clear no.
