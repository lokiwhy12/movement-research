# Gradient entry / low-friction front door

Definition
--
Gradient entry / low-friction front door is the mechanic where a movement gives prospects an easy first step that does not require immediate full-doctrine buy-in. The opening move might be an open meeting, self-test, intro book, workshop, trial practice, or beginner service. The point is to let people cross the first boundary under low pressure, get a small win or recognition event, and only then introduce stronger vocabulary, deeper rituals, and heavier commitment.

One-paragraph example
--
AA uses a relatively soft, non-salesy version. A crisis, referral, or simple willingness gets someone into a meeting, where the first ask is not money or worldview submission but return behavior: hear parallel stories, recognize yourself, and come back. Scientology uses a more engineered version. Personality tests, intro books, seminars, missions, and beginner services create a front door that feels like self-help or performance improvement before full institutional commitment is visible. Same primitive, different power geometry. AA uses gradient entry to reduce stigma and let commitment emerge through repeated return, sponsor attachment, and step work. Scientology uses it to create early wins that bridge prospects toward auditing, doctrinal adoption, and laddered escalation.

Why it matters
--
- Cuts entry friction for people who are curious, ashamed, skeptical, or not yet ready for total buy-in.
- Lets a movement prove subjective usefulness before asking for stronger sacrifice.
- Converts recognition or early relief into momentum for deeper identity change.
- Makes later escalation easier because the prospect has already crossed one reversible boundary.
- Reveals an ethical fork: honest sampling can lower fear, while disguised funneling can hide the true cost structure until commitment is already underway.

Shadow risk
--
This primitive turns dark when the front door is honest about the first step but misleading about the later structure. Then prospects enter a system they think is lightweight, optional, or narrowly therapeutic, only to discover stronger control, escalating cost, or identity capture after they've already invested attention, language, and trust. In soft systems this becomes a vague bait-and-switch. In hard systems it becomes a funnel that hides the real ladder until the prospect is already climbing it.

Safe translation
--
If a product or community borrows this mechanic, keep the first step truthful, reversible, and legible. Let the sample experience lower fear without disguising what deeper participation actually involves. Show the next layers clearly, keep exit costs low, and avoid using small wins as a pretext for hidden escalation. The useful mechanic is an easy, honest first crossing. The dangerous mechanic is a soft front door built to smuggle people into a harder system than they knowingly chose.
