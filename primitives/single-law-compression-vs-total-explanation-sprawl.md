# Single-law compression / total-explanation sprawl

Definition
--
Single-law compression / total-explanation sprawl is the mechanic where a movement decides how much explanatory architecture followers must carry. In the compressed form, one small master principle is presented as the hidden key that explains many outcomes at once. In the sprawling form, the worldview expands into an ever-growing interpretive web that can absorb new events, symbols, enemies, and apparent contradictions without losing coherence from the inside. Both reduce uncertainty. The difference is whether the movement wins by making reality feel elegantly simple or by making reality feel secretly connected at unlimited scale.

One-paragraph example
--
*The Secret* runs on single-law compression. Its packet says one hidden principle — the law of attraction — can explain money, love, health, and happiness if thought and feeling are aligned correctly. QAnon runs on total-explanation sprawl. Its packet frames politics, media, trafficking panic, public health distrust, and failed predictions as pieces of one hidden war that must be continuously decoded by the swarm. *The Secret* says, `carry this one law and apply it everywhere.` QAnon says, `keep widening the map until everything fits inside the plot.` One reduces complexity into a portable mantra. The other metabolizes complexity by turning endless interpretation into the lifestyle.

Why it matters
--
- Reveals the doctrine-loading strategy: compressed systems travel through memorability, while sprawling systems travel through interpretive depth and endless novelty.
- Changes onboarding friction: a one-law system is easy to explain in a sentence; a sprawling system often needs guides, lore summaries, and repeated translation.
- Changes retention behavior: compressed systems survive by repeat application; sprawling systems survive by absorbing contradiction into a larger hidden map.
- Shapes operator demand: single-law systems can run with low interpreter overhead, while total-explanation systems generate a standing need for decoders, explainers, and narrative stitchers.
- Marks a safety boundary: when a worldview can annex any new fact without real loss, disconfirmation starts to fail.

Shadow risk
--
Single-law compression can still be dangerous because it oversimplifies reality, encourages magical thinking, and can route failure back into the follower as a personal defect. Total-explanation sprawl is more corrosive. It can turn every new event into evidence, every contradiction into deeper mystery, and every correction into part of the plot. The risk is not just complexity or simplicity by itself. The risk is any explanatory system that keeps emotional certainty high while making real falsification harder.

Safe translation
--
If a product or community borrows from this mechanic, keep explanatory models bounded and scoped. Simple principles can be useful heuristics, but they should stay explicitly partial and reality-tested. Avoid any lore system that can swallow unrelated domains just to preserve emotional coherence. Healthy systems name what their model does **not** explain, publish correction triggers, and make it normal to retire a frame that stops matching the world.

Small-risk-mitigation checklist
--
- Treat simple principles as tools, not universal laws.
- Name explicit domain boundaries for any explanatory model.
- Publish what evidence would count against the frame.
- Do not let interpreters gain status by endlessly expanding the map.
- Watch for drift where `everything fits` starts replacing `what actually happened?`
- Preserve a normal path for saying, `this model no longer explains enough to keep using.`
