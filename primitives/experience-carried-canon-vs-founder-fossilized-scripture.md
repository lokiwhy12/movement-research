# Experience-carried canon / founder-fossilized scripture

Definition
--
Experience-carried canon / founder-fossilized scripture is the mechanic where a movement decides how its source authority survives scale and founder absence. On one end, a small canon stays alive because members keep reading it aloud, translating it through testimony, and re-embedding it in sponsor, room, or peer contexts. The text matters, but it stays socially metabolized through lived experience. On the other end, authority is fossilized into a founder-authored scripture set whose exact wording, recordings, procedures, and approved interpretations become the permanent standard. The same continuity problem is being solved in both cases: how do you preserve a method after the founder is gone? Experience-carried canon solves it through repeated communal reuse. Founder-fossilized scripture solves it through textual canonization, exactness, and institutional enforcement.

One-paragraph example
--
AA leans toward experience-carried canon. The Big Book, Steps, and Traditions matter, but they are continually reinstalled through meetings, read-aloud literature, sponsorship, group conscience, and the testimony of long-timers rather than through a centralized claim that one founder corpus must be applied with technical exactness in every life domain. Scientology leans toward founder-fossilized scripture. Its packet explicitly says Hubbard’s written and recorded words are the scripture, doctrine compression sits on top of tens of millions of founder-authored words, and post-founder leadership is framed as stewardship of exact Hubbard tech rather than as legitimate doctrinal adaptation. Same continuity challenge, opposite answer. AA keeps the canon alive by having people carry it. Scientology keeps the founder alive by turning his corpus into the permanent operating authority.

Why it matters
--
- Determines whether a movement feels socially interpreted or textually locked once the founder is gone.
- Changes adaptability: experience-carried canon can absorb local reality faster, while founder-fossilized scripture preserves sharper continuity at the cost of flexibility.
- Concentrates authority differently: living fellowship interpretation distributes power across rooms, sponsors, and norms; fossilized scripture pushes power toward official custodians, trainers, and orthodoxy enforcers.
- Shapes drift risk: experience-carried systems can dilute; founder-fossilized systems can calcify and become anti-correction machines.
- Reveals whether continuity is trusted to community memory or to institutional custody of the founder’s words.

Shadow risk
--
This primitive breaks both ways. Experience-carried canon can drift into folk distortion, selective memory, local capture by charismatic veterans, or quiet dilution where the original method becomes whatever the room currently prefers. Founder-fossilized scripture fails harder: the institution can treat founder text as untouchable even when reality changes, which makes error correction look like betrayal and gives official interpreters enormous power. The key question is whether the canon exists to keep the method recognizable or to freeze a founder’s authority beyond criticism.

Safe translation
--
If a product, community, or movement borrows this mechanic, keep a compact canon but force it to stay in conversation with lived practice. Use a few stable core texts or principles, teach them through repeated communal use, and preserve explicit pathways for correction when reality changes. Avoid building a system where fidelity to the founder’s wording outranks actual outcomes. The useful pattern is durable source language plus living interpretation. The dangerous pattern is founder preservation becoming more sacred than truth-testing.

Small-risk-mitigation checklist
--
- Keep the canon compact enough that ordinary members can actually reuse it without specialist gatekeepers.
- Require lived-example interpretation alongside text fidelity so the method stays reality-tested.
- Publish a correction path: if practice and outcomes diverge from doctrine, there must be a legitimate way to revise interpretation.
- Do not let founder language become a shield against feedback, evidence, or local truth.
- Separate respect for origin from total authority capture by official custodians of the canon.
- Audit whether trainers, senior members, or staff are helping people use the canon or simply enforcing reverence toward it.
