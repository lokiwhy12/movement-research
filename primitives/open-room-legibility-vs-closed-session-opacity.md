# Open-room legibility / closed-session opacity

Definition
--
Open-room legibility / closed-session opacity is the mechanic where a movement decides how inspectable its core ritual life will be to a newcomer, outsider, or ordinary member. On one end, the central practice happens in rooms whose format is publicly describable, socially legible, and easy to observe before deep commitment: people can attend, hear the language, watch the rhythm, and understand the basic bargain in real time. On the other end, the decisive work happens in private or semi-private sessions: trained-operator-mediated, confidentiality-framed containers whose procedures, meanings, or higher-stage significance are harder to inspect from the outside. Same trust problem, different answer. Open-room legibility makes the movement feel human-scale, reproducible, and easier to evaluate before surrendering authority. Closed-session opacity makes the movement feel specialized, serious, and institutionally controlled, but also increases information asymmetry and dependence on credentialed interpreters.

One-paragraph example
--
AA leans toward open-room legibility. The prospect can enter a meeting, hear readings and shares, see the room format, and understand the movement’s social logic before anything like Step 5 intimacy or long-term sponsorship becomes central. Scientology leans much harder toward closed-session opacity. Its core transformation rituals are private auditing sessions, exact training/checksheet environments, ethics/confessional handling, and higher teachings that are progressively gated by level and authority. Same question, opposite answer. AA says the method should become believable in the room, through ordinary repeated witness that is visible before major surrender. Scientology says the system becomes real inside specialized sessions and staged disclosure, where trust, payment, and interpretive dependence can deepen before the full method is broadly inspectable.

Why it matters
--
- Determines whether a newcomer can evaluate the social and ritual bargain before deep buy-in.
- Changes the abuse surface: inspectable rooms distribute observation, while opaque sessions raise the power of operators, institutions, and private unverifiable claims.
- Shapes replication: open-room systems can spread horizontally through simple room formats, while closed-session systems need stronger credentialing, scheduling, and institutional containment.
- Changes proof dynamics: open rooms rely more on ordinary mutual witness, while opaque systems rely more on private subjective breakthroughs and institutional testimony about what happened.
- Reveals what the movement trusts most: shared visibility and repeatability, or controlled delivery inside specialized containers.

Examples (evidence anchors)
--
- Alcoholics Anonymous packet: the format line is explicitly `in-person and online meetings, sponsorship dyads, literature, service structure`, showing that the basic movement container is legible at room level rather than hidden behind specialist delivery (`docs/targets/alcoholics-anonymous.target-packet.md`).
- Alcoholics Anonymous packet: the conversion path says the prospect enters `an open or closed meeting and encounters the room format, readings, and shares`, showing that early understanding comes through observable group ritual before higher-escalation commitments (`docs/targets/alcoholics-anonymous.target-packet.md`).
- Alcoholics Anonymous packet: the packet notes that `nonalcoholics can attend open meetings`, reinforcing that at least part of the method is intentionally inspectable from outside the in-group (`docs/targets/alcoholics-anonymous.target-packet.md`).
- Alcoholics Anonymous packet: meeting guidance describes a chair opening the format, literature being read aloud, and speaker meetings centered on `what we were like, what happened, and what we are like now`, showing a publicly intelligible ritual sequence carried in ordinary rooms (`docs/targets/alcoholics-anonymous.target-packet.md`).
- Scientology packet: the format line names `auditing sessions, training, ethics actions, graded advancement ladder, staff and Sea Org tracks, large event broadcasts`, showing that core transformation is routed through institutional containers rather than only through open peer rooms (`docs/targets/scientology.target-packet.md`).
- Scientology packet: official auditing is described as spiritual counseling using exact questions/directions and E-Meter ritual, showing that the core change mechanism is private, operator-mediated, and harder for outsiders to independently inspect (`docs/targets/scientology.target-packet.md`).
- Scientology packet: the packet explicitly identifies `information partitioning` and `higher teachings` as part of a managed knowledge environment, showing that full evaluation of the system is deliberately staged rather than front-loaded (`docs/targets/scientology.target-packet.md`).
- Scientology packet: the packet states that the structure can create a `powerful asymmetry because the organization can know far more about the member than the member knows about the organization`, showing the control risk that appears when core ritual life becomes opaque (`docs/targets/scientology.target-packet.md`).

Shadow risk
--
Open-room legibility can fail by becoming too shallow, too exposed, or too socially noisy for vulnerable work; people may self-censor if every meaningful step is fully public. Closed-session opacity fails harder: unverifiable claims can hide behind seriousness, privacy can blur into secrecy, operator authority can inflate beyond accountability, and members can be asked to trust containers they cannot really evaluate. The key question is whether privacy is protecting the member or protecting the institution from scrutiny.

Safe translation
--
If a product, community, or movement borrows this mechanic, make the core container inspectable by default. Let newcomers see the basic ritual, language, and social bargain before major payment, trust transfer, or identity surrender is required. Reserve private sessions for bounded support, not for making the real method legible only after dependence has already formed. Publish facilitation standards, clarify what is confidential vs what is merely proprietary, and keep the center of gravity in ordinary reproducible rooms rather than in specialist black boxes. The useful pattern is transparent group ritual plus optional protected dyads. The dangerous pattern is making the true bargain visible only from inside the closed chamber.

Small-risk-mitigation checklist
--
- Keep the basic ritual bargain visible before major payment, deep disclosure, or identity transfer is requested.
- Publish what a newcomer can expect inside the room or session, including what is observable, what is confidential, and what is gated.
- Use private sessions for bounded support, not as the only place where the real method becomes intelligible.
- Let members inspect facilitator standards, escalation paths, and complaint routes without needing insider status.
- Audit for opacity drift: if important claims can only be validated inside closed containers, the design is becoming dependency-prone.
- Make it possible to remain in the legible, group-level layer without penalty, status loss, or coercive upsell into private chambers.
