# Self-stabilizing service loop

Definition
--
Self-stabilizing service loop is the mechanic where a movement makes helping, sponsoring, staffing, or disseminating part of how a member stays aligned themselves. Service is not framed as a bonus layer that appears after transformation. It is built into the transformation engine. The member keeps or deepens their own identity partly by carrying the method to the next person, holding the room, or doing work for the system.

One-paragraph example
--
AA uses this in a relatively healthy, low-theater form. Step 12, sponsorship, chairing meetings, hospital/prison carries, and other service roles all teach the same lesson: helping another alcoholic is not separate from staying sober; it is one of the ways sobriety is maintained. Scientology runs a much harder version. Dissemination is framed as helping mankind, mission/church roles turn service into institutional throughput, and Sea Org commitment dramatizes total-life labor in the name of planetary clearing. Same primitive, different moral temperature. In both systems, the member is stabilized by outward service. AA uses that loop to turn recovery into usefulness and fellowship continuity. Scientology uses it to fuse usefulness with institutional expansion, doctrinal loyalty, and escalating labor commitment.

Why it matters
--
- Turns retention into contribution instead of leaving veteran members as passive consumers.
- Makes distribution feel morally meaningful rather than merely promotional.
- Gives members a way to convert gratitude, conviction, or improvement into identity-stabilizing action.
- Helps a movement reproduce itself because service activity also carries doctrine, ritual, and norms.
- Reveals a major design fork: service can build stewardship and belonging, or it can become sanctified labor extraction.

- Alcoholics Anonymous packet: service ritual and Twelfth Step work turn helping, chairing, sponsoring, and carrying meetings into recurring practices that convert recipients into operators and make the fellowship self-replicating (`shared/docs/targets/alcoholics-anonymous.target-packet.md`).
- Alcoholics Anonymous packet: retention mechanics explicitly name service as a primary lever that keeps members engaged after the beginner phase and ties sobriety to responsibility and usefulness (`shared/docs/targets/alcoholics-anonymous.target-packet.md`).
- Scientology packet: loyalty and zeal mechanisms explicitly frame dissemination as helping mankind / clearing the planet, turning outward propagation into evidence of conviction and belonging (`shared/docs/targets/scientology.target-packet.md`).

Shadow risk
--
This primitive goes bad when a movement makes usefulness the price of belonging. Then service stops being stewardship and becomes moralized labor extraction. Members can start feeling that rest, doubt, or reduced output equals regression, betrayal, or spiritual failure. In softer systems, this produces burnout and hidden resentment. In harder systems, it produces totalizing obligation, recruitment pressure, and the inability to tell where care ends and institutional exploitation begins.

Safe translation
--
If a product or community borrows this mechanic, keep service bounded, visible, and consent-based. Let members contribute in ways that deepen agency, skill, and belonging without making output the proof of worth. Separate care roles from recruitment quotas. Build off-ramps, rotation, and explicit rest norms so stewardship does not quietly mutate into compulsory labor. The useful mechanic is identity stabilized through contribution. The dangerous mechanic is converting contribution into sacred debt.
