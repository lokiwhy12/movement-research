# Ordinary-desire sanctification / righteous-duty mobilization

Definition
--
Ordinary-desire sanctification / righteous-duty mobilization is the mechanic where a movement decides what should feel morally legitimate at the center of participation. In the ordinary-desire form, the system gives people permission to want more for themselves: money, love, health, confidence, status, relief, beauty, or a better life. Desire is not treated as selfish drift but as evidence of alignment, possibility, or rightful expansion. In the righteous-duty form, the system redirects attention away from private aspiration and toward a larger cause, emergency, or moral obligation. Participation feels serious because the member is not merely improving a life but helping protect children, expose evil, save the nation, defend truth, or carry a collective mission. Both forms are adhesive. The difference is whether the movement keeps people by blessing personal desire or by conscripting them into duty.

One-paragraph example
--
*The Secret* leans hard toward ordinary-desire sanctification. Its packet says you can `have, do, or be anything you want,` maps the promise directly onto abundance, love, health, and wealth, and explicitly notes that the system `turns desire into virtue` by reframing wanting more as alignment rather than greed. QAnon leans hard toward righteous-duty mobilization. Its packet centers child-protection framing, patriot identity, participatory decoding duty, and mission behavior that makes attention itself feel like a moral obligation. Same adhesion problem, opposite motivational center. *The Secret* tells the follower that pursuing a better life is spiritually valid. QAnon tells the follower that staying engaged is a duty in the face of hidden evil.

Why it matters
--
- Reveals the moral fuel source: some movements grow by legitimizing personal aspiration, while others grow by converting attention into obligation.
- Changes recruitment tone: desire systems sound hopeful, permissive, and possibility-rich; duty systems sound urgent, righteous, and morally loaded.
- Changes what retention feels like: desire systems reward continued self-focus and private benefit imagination, while duty systems reward vigilance, sacrifice, and contribution to a larger cause.
- Changes upsell shape: sanctified-desire systems can expand easily into more life domains, while duty systems expand into more evidence feeds, actions, communities, and battle narratives.
- Marks a safety boundary: both poles can drift into manipulation when the system either flatters every wanting as cosmic truth or makes disengagement feel like moral failure.

Shadow risk
--
Both poles can become distorting in different ways. Desire-sanctifying systems can slide into magical consumer narcissism, where ordinary wants are treated as self-validating cosmic signals and external constraint gets quietly minimized. Duty-mobilizing systems fail differently: they can turn attention into permanent obligation, reward self-erasure in the name of the cause, and make stepping back feel selfish or complicit. The danger is not personal ambition or shared mission by themselves. The danger is building a system that can only keep people by either flattering desire beyond proportion or loading every pause with guilt.

Safe translation
--
If a product or community borrows anything from this mechanic, let people pursue meaningful personal outcomes without pretending every desire is sacred or every cause is an emergency. It is fine to help members name what they want, and it is fine to invite contribution to something larger. The safe pattern is balance: private benefit goals that remain reality-checked, plus shared mission language that remains bounded, voluntary, and proportional. The dangerous pattern is forcing members to choose between self-expansion without limits and duty without off-ramps.

Small-risk-mitigation checklist
--
- Do not treat every strong desire as proof that a person is `meant` to pursue it.
- Do not treat every shared mission as morally urgent by default.
- Keep private-goal language tied to reality, tradeoffs, and evidence.
- Keep mission language voluntary, bounded, and compatible with stepping back.
- Watch for guilt-based retention: either `you are blocking your abundance` or `you are abandoning the cause.`
- Preserve room for ordinary limits, competing priorities, and non-participation without shame.
