# Trusted-referral intake / algorithmic feed capture

Definition
--
Trusted-referral intake / algorithmic feed capture is the mechanic where a movement decides what substrate does the first conversion work. On one end, people enter mainly through intentional human channels: a friend referral, a workshop invite, a coach, a retreat, a story from someone they know, or a clearly bounded media piece that routes into a named container. On the other end, the movement lets platform systems do much of the intake work. Discovery happens through recommendation feeds, softened hashtags, influencer explainers, adjacent grievance content, repost chains, and ambient social drift. The same growth problem is being solved in both cases: how do you get a newcomer to first contact? Trusted-referral intake solves it through social trust and deliberate handoff. Algorithmic feed capture solves it by embedding the movement inside the attention machinery people are already swimming in.

One-paragraph example
--
NXIVM and OneTaste both leaned more toward trusted-referral intake, even though they used different wrappers. NXIVM grew through referral, word of mouth, leadership-development positioning, and live intensives that people knowingly stepped into. OneTaste mixed referrals, media intrigue, workshops, retreats, and community stories that still routed people into a recognizable event or branded practice container. QAnon leaned toward algorithmic feed capture. People often met it through platform-native discovery: softened anti-trafficking slogans, influencer translation, adjacent wellness or anti-elite content, family social feeds, and hashtag hijacking that let the worldview ride existing attention streams. Same acquisition problem, opposite substrate. NXIVM and OneTaste usually asked someone to cross a deliberate threshold. QAnon let the feed do much of the first conversion work.

Why it matters
--
- Determines whether first contact happens inside a chosen container or through ambient drift across mainstream attention surfaces.
- Changes accountability: referral-led intake leaves clearer provenance and human responsibility, while algorithmic capture diffuses responsibility across platforms, interpreters, and repost chains.
- Affects growth speed and correction difficulty: feed capture can scale fast and keep re-serving the worldview even after partial disconfirmation.
- Explains why some systems can spread without strong formal organization; the platform itself becomes part of the acquisition engine.
- Reveals a safety boundary: if the movement depends on people stumbling into it through softened or unlabeled adjacent content, informed consent is already degraded at entry.

Shadow risk
--
This primitive fails on both sides. Trusted-referral intake can still become coercive when relationships are used as leverage, when social trust outruns honest disclosure, or when referral pressure becomes part of the member role. Algorithmic feed capture fails more diffusely and more explosively. It lets a movement bypass deliberate threshold choice, blur where the content is really coming from, and keep reacquiring attention through platform incentives that reward outrage, mystery, or moral panic. The danger is not just scale. It is that the newcomer may already be partially recruited before they ever recognize that a bounded system is recruiting them.

Safe translation
--
If a product, community, or movement borrows anything from this mechanic, keep the intake path explicit and provenance-rich. Let discovery happen through clear invitations, honest storytelling, searchable public materials, and opt-in channels that name the real system. Do not rely on unlabeled adjacent content, hijacked hashtags, or opaque recommendation loops to do the first conversion work for you. The useful pattern is friction-aware invitation with clear source identity. The dangerous pattern is stealth acquisition through ambient feed contamination.

Small-risk-mitigation checklist
--
- Name the real system clearly in discovery surfaces and first-contact materials.
- Prefer opt-in channels, explicit referrals, and bounded event pages over stealth distribution through unrelated discourse streams.
- Audit whether growth depends on people knowingly choosing the container or merely being repeatedly exposed inside algorithmic feeds.
- Do not hijack unrelated cause hashtags, crises, or public anxieties to manufacture discovery.
- Keep a clear correction path so mistaken or misleading top-of-funnel content can be fixed quickly.
- Treat any growth loop that works better when provenance is fuzzy as a structural warning sign.
