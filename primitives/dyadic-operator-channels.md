# Dyadic operator channels

Definition
--
Dyadic operator channels are recurring one-to-one relationships where a more initiated member (sponsor, coach, guide, trainer) becomes the movement's smallest durable transmission unit. Instead of relying only on mass events, the system keeps the worldview alive through repeated private contact that translates doctrine into next actions, accountability, and emotional holding.

One-paragraph example
--
AA uses the sponsor-sponsee dyad as a recovery relay: the newcomer does not just attend meetings, they ask someone further along to help them work the steps, stay reachable between meetings, and model what sober life looks like in practice. OneTaste ran a more commercial and status-bearing version through coaches, teachers, and trainers who moved participants from first workshop curiosity into repeat practice, retreats, and higher-commitment lanes. CrossFit shows a softer mainstream variant: the local coach is not a confessor or life-guide, but repeated class contact, scaling decisions, score visibility, and coach recognition still make the operator-member lane a real transmission channel. Different ethics, same primitive: a one-to-one operator channel keeps the movement warm between group containers, personalizes the next move, and makes the system feel held by a person rather than only by an institution.

Why it matters
--
- Bridges the gap between flagship events or group meetings and everyday life.
- Translates abstract doctrine into concrete next actions for one person at a time.
- Makes retention less dependent on mass charisma because transmission happens locally.
- Lets the movement reproduce through human relays instead of only through official media or events.
- Concentrates trust quickly, which is powerful in both healthy-support and high-control systems.

Examples (evidence anchors)
--
- AA packet: sponsorship is a core peer-guidance and accountability lane inside the fellowship, explicitly described as continuous sharing of experience and a structured support bridge between meetings (`docs/targets/alcoholics-anonymous.target-packet.md`).
- OneTaste packet: coach / teacher / trainer roles, coaching-program escalation, and repeat-practice lanes turn participant energy into recurring authority channels (`shared/docs/targets/onetaste.target-packet.md`).
- CrossFit packet: credentialed coaches, class-based scaling, and recurring whiteboard/recognition culture show a softer mainstream version where coach contact still carries norms, accountability, and belonging (`docs/targets/crossfit.target-packet.md`).

Shadow risk
--
Dyadic channels become dangerous when support slides into dependency, secrecy, or unreviewable authority. A one-to-one lane can magnify pressure, blur consent, enable manipulation, or turn the operator into the real source of truth. The risk spikes when money, status, sexuality, confession, or moral judgment are fused into the dyad.

Safe translation
--
If a product or community uses dyadic support, keep the role bounded, transparent, and easy to exit. Members should be able to reassign guides, escalate concerns, and rely on written norms rather than the private will of a mentor. The operator should help someone re-enter the broader practice with more agency, not trap them inside a dependency loop.

Small-risk-mitigation checklist
--
- Keep mentor or sponsor roles explicitly scoped; no private expansion into total-life control by default.
- Make reassignment easy and stigma-free if the fit is bad.
- Separate guidance from sexual access, upsell pressure, and confessional leverage.
- Publish the role norms so members can compare private guidance against the written standard.
- Ensure the dyad routes people back into the broader group and practice, not away from it.
- Provide a clear escalation path when a guide becomes coercive, dependency-producing, or boundary-blurring.
- Treat operator usefulness as measured by member agency growth, not member obedience.
